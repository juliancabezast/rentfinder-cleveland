Rental Registration, Occupancy Certificates and Leasing Permits: What They Are and What They Cost

There is no CMHA landlord license — CMHA states it has no pre-approval process for landlords or units. The permit is municipal. CMHA must collect a Certificate of Occupancy and/or Rental Registration with the RFTA in 17 named Cuyahoga County suburbs, and since October 1, 2025 it verifies City of Cleveland rental registrations through the City's Citizen Access Portal.

A permit from your city, not from CMHA

Owners ask this as a license question, and it splits in two. CMHA licenses nobody: its Leasing with CMHA page states, "Please note that CMHA does not have a pre-approval process for landlords or units," and its Landlord FAQ answers "How do I qualify as an HCVP Landlord?" with "No application process is required."

The permit, where one exists, comes from the municipality — which is why CMHA's phrasing stays open. It asks for a copy of the "Certificate of Occupancy and/or Rental Registration." Which document your city issues, and what it takes to get one, is set locally. In Cleveland it carries a third name: a Certificate Approving Rental Occupancy.

Why CMHA cares whether you have one

In part of its service area CMHA cannot move your file without one. It must collect a copy of the Certificate of Occupancy and/or Rental Registration when a Request for Tenancy Approval is submitted for a unit in 17 named suburbs: Bedford, Bedford Heights, Brook Park, Cleveland Heights, Cuyahoga Heights, East Cleveland, Euclid, Fairview Park, Garfield Heights, Lakewood, Lyndhurst, Maple Heights, Parma, Shaker Heights, South Euclid, University Heights and Warrensville Heights.

For Cleveland units it runs a lookup instead: "Effective October 1, 2025, CMHA will verify active City of Cleveland rental registrations through the City's Citizen Access Portal for units located in Cleveland." A lapsed registration reads as lapsed.

It matters: CMHA reviews the RFTA for completeness and will not accept missing information or corrections by phone: deficiencies must be corrected in writing, an ordinary way to lose a week.

What Cleveland's Residents First rules require

Under Residents First, owners of non-owner-occupied residential property must register and pay registration fees for each property, and submit an affidavit naming a local agent in charge, proof of current taxes or an established payment plan, and lead-safe certification, to obtain a Certificate Approving Rental Occupancy. The City's warning: "[f]ailure to register rental properties as required may result in penalties, fines, and possible criminal prosecution."

The local agent must be based in Cuyahoga County and takes legal responsibility for the property; Signal Cleveland reports enforcement by $200 civil tickets whose unpaid fines are added to the property tax bill. As of mid-June 2025, landlords representing 10,314 properties had registered 2,433 local agents.

The tax condition lands twice. Cleveland wants proof of current taxes or a payment plan, and CMHA separately verifies ownership through the County Auditor's website, confirming the unit is not in foreclosure and is current on taxes.

The lead-safe certificate inside it

Cleveland requires owners of rental units built before 1978 to hold a Lead Safe Certification or an exemption, valid for two years, after which owners must re-apply; owner-occupied properties do not need to be certified. Because it is a Residents First prerequisite for the occupancy certificate, that two-year clock is a clock on renting the unit.

It is the only piece here with a published expiry date — see Lead Paint Rules for Cleveland Rentals.

What it costs

Three bills; we can quote only one.

The city's. Cleveland requires owners to register and pay registration fees for each property, and the fee and the process are set by the municipality, not by CMHA. We found no consolidated Greater Cleveland table of those amounts — so we pull the current figure from the relevant building department for the specific address rather than repeat a stale one.

Ours, fixed and published:

  • Tenant placement — one month's rent, charged only after the tenant is placed. No placement, no fee
  • Inspection attendance with a written report — $100. Attend yourself instead and pay nothing
  • Ongoing management — 5% of monthly rent, only while the unit is rented
  • Repair estimates — free, itemized, back within 24 hours. Nothing starts without your approval

The third bill: not having one

CMHA pays on the 3rd and the 16th, and on a new contract states you will be paid "on the next scheduled payment date after HCVP has received three copies of the signed contracts, the executed lease and occupancy permit, when applicable" — the occupancy permit is named inside CMHA's own payment rule. The outer limit is hard: the HAP contract "must be executed no later than 60 calendar days from the beginning of the lease term," and "[a]ny HAP contract executed after the 60-day period is void."

Do not bridge the gap with keys. CMHA warns that if the owner permits the tenant to move in early, "the tenant will be responsible for the full amount of the rent and HCVP will not pay the back amount retroactively." Meanwhile the unit sits empty: at HUD's FY2026 two-bedroom Fair Market Rent of $1,279 for the Cleveland metro, about $42 a day in lost gross rent ($1,279 ÷ 30 = $42.63) — our arithmetic on HUD's figure, not a published statistic.

Do it before the RFTA, not after

Registration is one of the few items you can finish with no tenant, no inspection date and no rent offer in hand. The other is CMHA's Mandatory Landlord Orientation — required since January 1, 2026 for the owner or designated managing agent, every 24 months, before a HAP contract can be executed for a new tenancy.

After that the clocks stop being yours. CMHA's Landlord Participation Guide describes the voucher as expiring 180 days from the issue date, and CMHA policy allows a family one live request: "The family may not submit, and the PHA will not process, more than one (1) RTA at a time." While your certificate is chased, that household cannot pursue another unit. See Why Your RFTA Is Stuck, and the Cuyahoga County map for who requires what.

Where we come in

In the City of Cleveland source of income is not a protected class — vouchers are absent from the City's list of thirteen — so an owner there may lawfully decline one. Registration is not a voucher rule; it is the condition of renting in that city at all.

We work Greater Cleveland and Cuyahoga County under CMHA, and we have completed CMHA's Virtual Landlord Orientation — the step that must be done before a HAP contract can be executed on a new tenancy. On registration we do the unglamorous part: confirm what the municipality requires for that address, get the current fee from its building department, and get the documents filed before the RFTA goes in.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a rental license to rent my house in Ohio?
Not from CMHA — it states it has no pre-approval process for landlords or units, and its FAQ says no application process is required. The permit is municipal: CMHA collects a Certificate of Occupancy and/or Rental Registration with the RFTA in 17 named Cuyahoga County suburbs, and since October 1, 2025 verifies City of Cleveland registrations through the City's portal.
What does Cleveland require before it issues a rental occupancy certificate?
Under Residents First, owners of non-owner-occupied residential property must register and pay registration fees for each property and submit an affidavit naming a local agent in charge, proof of current taxes or an established payment plan, and lead-safe certification. The City states that failure to register as required may result in penalties, fines and possible criminal prosecution.
How much does rental registration cost?
Cleveland requires owners to register and pay registration fees per property, and the fee and the process are set by the municipality, not by CMHA. What happens if my registration has lapsed when I file the RFTA?
The file stalls before payment is ever in question, and CMHA will not accept RFTA corrections by phone. On a new contract CMHA pays on the next scheduled date — the 3rd or the 16th — after receiving the signed contracts, executed lease and occupancy permit. The HAP contract must be executed within 60 calendar days of the lease term beginning, or it is void.
