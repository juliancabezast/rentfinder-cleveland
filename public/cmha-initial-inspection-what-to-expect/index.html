The CMHA Move-In Inspection: Scheduling, Clocks and Second Chances

Submitting the RFTA triggers the initial inspection. CMHA policy is to inspect, decide and notify owner and family within 15 days of RFTA submission, with the clock suspended whenever the unit is unavailable. A failed unit is rescheduled within 7 to 10 days. Fail that second inspection and the family is told in writing to find another unit.

The clock starts at the RFTA, not at your phone call

CMHA has no landlord application and no pre-approval process — its Leasing with CMHA page states that "CMHA does not have a pre-approval process for landlords or units." Inspection is step 6 of CMHA's seven published steps, and only a submitted RFTA packet schedules it. You can file it: "[t]he owner may submit the RTA on behalf of the family," by email to rfta@cmha.net or at the kiosks in CMHA's Main Campus lobby.

Because the clock runs from submission, an incomplete packet burns days you never get back. CMHA reviews the RFTA for completeness and consistency with the proposed lease and will not accept missing information or corrections by phone. Seventeen named suburbs also require a Certificate of Occupancy or Rental Registration with the packet. Six things that stall an RFTA covers that ground.

Fifteen days to inspect, decide and notify — with one pause button

CMHA policy is to complete the initial inspection, determine whether the unit passes, and notify owner and family "within 15 days of submission of the Request for Tenancy Approval (RTA)," with the clock suspended for any period the unit is not available for inspection.

Read that second half twice: it is the part you control. A unit that cannot be entered or evaluated is not simply inspected later — it stops the clock while the family's voucher keeps aging. CMHA's 24-hour emergency list includes no water, no electricity and no heat between October 15 and April 15, so utilities shut off is not a neutral condition. If a prior tenant is still in place, R.C. 5321.04(A)(8) presumes 24 hours to be reasonable notice before entry.

Inspections are run by an outside firm CMHA contracts with, against the 13 key housing quality aspects. Scheduling goes to 216.478.9680; inspection help is 216.271.2116. There is no fast lane: CMHA publishes no expedited, fast-track, pre-inspection or self-certification option for landlords.

Every published clock, in the order you meet it

Every clock in the CMHA inspection process is a policy target from its FY2025 Administrative Plan and live landlord pages, not a measured average — CMHA publishes no turnaround statistics. Plan for longer.

Two rows deserve emphasis. The tenancy-approval determination is a separate decision from the inspection result: a unit that passes still waits on it. And the five-business-day reinspection clock starts when you notify CMHA that the corrections are made, not when you finish the work.

Two federal rules help. Under 24 CFR 982.405(i) an authority verifying a correction "may accept photographic evidence or other reliable evidence from the owner" instead of another site visit — worth asking for on a small fix. Under 24 CFR 982.405(h) it may not charge you for the initial inspection, but may set "a reasonable fee" for a reinspection, which cannot be passed to the family. CMHA does not publish the amount.

Two attempts, then the family is told to find another unit

This is the answer most owners are looking for, and it is short. An initial inspection gets two attempts. A failed unit is rescheduled within 7 to 10 days, and CMHA policy is that "[w]hen a unit fails an initial inspection the second time, the HCVP applicant/participant is provided with written notice to find another unit."

That is tighter than the rest of the program: for periodic, emergency and special inspections, CMHA's Landlord Participation Guide says "Up to three inspections are allowed." The move-in visit is the narrowest margin CMHA gives you.

A second failure costs you the applicant, not just the appointment. A voucher expires 180 days from its issue date, and CMHA's landlord guide notes that a housing authority may bar a family from having more than one Request for Tenancy Approval outstanding at a time, so days spent on your unit can be days the family cannot spend elsewhere. CMHA told the U.S. Commission on Civil Rights that only about 50% of households admitted to the program in Cuyahoga County found housing before their voucher expired.

To see what failed, check the portal: CMHA's Landlord Participation Guide describes failed inspection items with photos appearing within 72 hours, though the guide is undated — confirm by phone.

What a pass unlocks — and the deadline waiting behind it

Passing is not the finish line; executing the HAP contract is. Under 24 CFR 982.305(c) the HAP contract has to be executed no later than 60 calendar days from the beginning of the lease term, and one executed after that period is void — with one narrow escape: where extenuating circumstances prevented the authority from meeting the deadline, it may ask the HUD field office for an extension, and HUD decides at its sole discretion. Do not plan around that. CMHA may pay nothing before execution, but a contract signed inside the 60 days pays for the portion of the lease term before execution, to a maximum of 60 days. What a HAP contract is walks through it.

One more gate closes the loop back to the RFTA. Since January 1, 2026, submitting an RFTA triggers a requirement that the owner or managing agent complete CMHA's Virtual Landlord Orientation — about 90 minutes, live and virtual, once every 24 months — before CMHA can execute a HAP contract for a new tenancy.

Finally, do not hand over keys early. CMHA warns that if the owner permits the tenant to move in before the unit passes and the contract is executed, "the tenant will be responsible for the full amount of the rent."

Frequently asked questions

How soon after the RFTA does CMHA inspect?
CMHA policy is to complete the initial inspection, decide pass or fail, and notify owner and family within 15 days of submission of the Request for Tenancy Approval — with the clock suspended for any period the unit is not available for inspection. That is a published target, not a measured average; CMHA publishes no turnaround data.
How many chances do I get to pass the initial inspection?
Two. A failed unit is rescheduled for a second inspection within 7 to 10 days, and CMHA reinspects within five business days of the date you notify it the corrections are made. CMHA policy is that when a unit fails an initial inspection the second time, the applicant is given written notice to find another unit.
What happens to the family if my unit fails twice?
They get written notice to find another unit, and the search restarts. A voucher expires 180 days from its issue date, and CMHA’s landlord guide notes a housing authority may limit a family to one Request for Tenancy Approval at a time, so days spent on your unit can be days unavailable elsewhere. Only about half of Cuyahoga County households admitted to the program leased up before expiration.
Can I ask CMHA to inspect sooner?
There is nothing published to ask for: CMHA offers no expedited, fast-track, pre-inspection or self-certification option for landlords. What you control is availability, because the 15-day clock is suspended for any period the unit is not available for inspection. Scheduling and inspection requests go to 216.478.9680; inspection help is 216.271.2116.
Can the tenant move in while we wait on the inspection?
CMHA warns that if the owner permits the tenant to move in before the unit passes and the contract is executed, "the tenant will be responsible for the full amount of the rent." No assistance is paid before execution, and a contract executed more than 60 calendar days after the lease begins is void unless HUD grants an extension for extenuating circumstances.
