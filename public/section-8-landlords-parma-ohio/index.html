Section 8 Landlords in Parma: Bungalows, Doubles and the City Occupancy Permit

Parma is one of 17 Cuyahoga County suburbs where CMHA must collect a Certificate of Occupancy and/or Rental Registration with the Request for Tenancy Approval. The document comes from City Hall, not from CMHA, and CMHA names an occupancy permit again in what it must receive before your first payment.

CMHA licenses nobody. Parma issues the document

Owners merge two systems into one imaginary permit. Only one is a license, and it is not CMHA's: "CMHA does not have a pre-approval process for landlords or units," and the Administrative Plan agrees — "The PHA does not formally approve an owner to participate in the HCV program."

What CMHA does in Parma is collect a document City Hall issues: a Certificate of Occupancy and/or Rental Registration, with the RFTA, for units in 17 named suburbs — Bedford, Bedford Heights, Brook Park, Cleveland Heights, Cuyahoga Heights, East Cleveland, Euclid, Fairview Park, Garfield Heights, Lakewood, Lyndhurst, Maple Heights, Parma, Shaker Heights, South Euclid, University Heights and Warrensville Heights.

Read the word *with*: it rides in the packet. Cleveland is the opposite — since October 1, 2025 CMHA verifies Cleveland registrations through the City's Citizen Access Portal instead. Cleveland owners attach nothing; Parma owners attach. Requirements differ by municipality: The Cuyahoga County Map.

The permit shows up a second time, at the pay window

Most owners file the certificate away as intake paperwork. CMHA names it again at the far end, describing when a first check moves: "you will be paid on the next scheduled payment date after HCVP has received three copies of the signed contracts, the executed lease and occupancy permit, when applicable."

Payment dates are "generally on the 3rd and 16th of each month," so a certificate that is missing, expired or in the wrong name costs you twice.

What we will not print about Parma's permit

Fees, renewal cadence, whether an inspection is attached, how far ahead to file — the City sets those and they move. Confirm the schedule with Parma's building department before you advertise.

And the rules quoted at you from Cleveland are Cleveland's: Residents First registration, the local-agent affidavit, the city lead-safe certificate. Do not assume they follow you to Parma — or that nothing does. See Rental Registration, Occupancy Certificates and Leasing Permits.

Bungalows and doubles: CMHA inspects the unit, not the building

On a double, each side is its own tenancy. The RFTA comes from the tenant, not from CMHA — though "[t]he owner may submit the RTA on behalf of the family." Two assisted tenancies means two packets, two inspections, two HAP contracts, two clocks.

Check the voucher's expiration date — "180 days from the issue date" — and its bedroom size. CMHA applies the payment standard for the smaller of voucher size or actual unit size, so the bigger half of a double buys no more subsidy.

Older stock fails on hardware. Since December 29, 2024 CMHA requires a smoke detector on each level, inside each bedroom or sleeping area, and within 21 feet of each bedroom door, all 10-year sealed and tamper-resistant or hardwired. Carbon monoxide detectors have been required since December 27, 2022 wherever there is a gas-fueled appliance, an attached garage or a wood-burning fireplace; lacking one is "an emergency fail subject to correction within 24 hours."

Paint carries its own clock. HUD treats "any deteriorated paint in target housing" as a violation of the inspection standards. Where a pre-1978 unit is cited for defective paint and a child under 6 will live there, CMHA allows 10 days from the initial move-in inspection — 30 from an annual — for a passed clearance test and proof of work by a certified lead abatement contractor.

The clocks that start when the packet goes in

CMHA publishes policy targets, not turnaround statistics.

  • The voucher is expiring throughout — 180 days from the issue date
  • CMHA will not accept missing information or corrections by phone
  • No early keys: the tenant "will be responsible for the full amount of the rent and HCVP will not pay the back amount retroactively"

Rent follows the ZIP code, not the city line

There is no single Parma number. CMHA sets payment standards per ZIP code under Small Area Fair Market Rents, effective January 1, 2026, and ZIP boundaries do not stop at city limits. The FY2026 two-bedroom standard runs from $1,096 in ZIP 44127 to $2,169 in 44113, so look your address up in CMHA's Rent Determination tool: CMHA Payment Standards by ZIP Code.

It caps subsidy, not rent: "The payment standard is NOT the maximum amount that the landlord can charge." Your number comes from rent reasonableness, run after the RFTA against comparable unassisted units and applied to gross rent — rent plus tenant-paid utilities or the allowance, not the figure you advertised.

Nobody in Parma can make you do this

The U.S. Commission on Civil Rights' Ohio Advisory Committee is blunt: "Source-of-income discrimination is legal in most of Ohio." Five Cuyahoga County municipalities ban it — Cleveland Heights, University Heights, South Euclid, Warrensville Heights and Linndale — and Parma is not among them. There is no county-wide ordinance.

So the case is worth-it, never obligation. CMHA's owner benefit list promises "[s]table, guaranteed rent payments deposited directly into your account" — CMHA's word, not ours: nothing is guaranteed, no unit is approved until it passes, and "HCVP does not pay for tenant caused damages." The scale is real: roughly 15,000 voucher holders in Cuyahoga County, 20,000 households on the waiting list, and — per CMHA's testimony — only about 50% of admitted households leasing before the voucher expired.

Get the certificate from the City of Parma first: CMHA collects it *with* the RFTA and wants an occupancy permit again before the first payment. Fix detectors and paint before the packet goes in; price off the ZIP code. Call (440) 444-4737, or read Section 8 Stress Free.

Frequently asked questions

Does Parma require a permit before CMHA will process my RFTA?
CMHA must collect a Certificate of Occupancy and/or Rental Registration with the Request for Tenancy Approval for units in 17 named suburbs, and Parma is one. The City issues it, not CMHA, which has no landlord application or pre-approval process. Get it before the packet goes in.
Why does CMHA mention an occupancy permit again before the first payment?
Because it sits on the list of what must arrive first: CMHA pays on the next scheduled payment date after HCVP has received three copies of the signed contracts, the executed lease and occupancy permit, when applicable. Payment dates are generally the 3rd and 16th.
Do both halves of a double need separate paperwork?
Each assisted tenancy carries its own Request for Tenancy Approval, signed by that family and by the owner, and its own initial inspection triggered when the packet is submitted. CMHA also limits a family to one live RFTA at a time, so a double means two of everything.
How long does CMHA take once the RFTA is in?
CMHA publishes policy targets, not turnaround statistics: inspect, decide and notify within 15 days of submission; complete the tenancy determination within 10 business days of receiving all required information; reinspect within five business days of your notice. The contract must be executed within 60 calendar days of the lease start.
Do I have to accept a housing voucher in Parma?
No. Ohio has no statewide source-of-income protection, and the federal Fair Housing Act does not cover a tenant's lawful income source. Five Cuyahoga County municipalities ban source-of-income discrimination — Cleveland Heights, University Heights, South Euclid, Warrensville Heights and Linndale — and Parma is not one.
