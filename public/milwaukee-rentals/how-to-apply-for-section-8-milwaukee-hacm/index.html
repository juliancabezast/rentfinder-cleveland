How to Apply for Section 8 in Milwaukee (HACM Step by Step)

As of August 8, 2026, HACM states that "The Section 8 tenant-based rent assistance waiting list is currently CLOSED" — but two doors are open. HACM says "WE ARE CURRENTLY ACCEPTING PORTABILITY," so a voucher issued elsewhere can be moved to Milwaukee, and the Public Housing and Project-Based Voucher waiting lists are open. If you are already on a list, HACM requires you to confirm continued interest through myportal.hacm.org by August 16, 2026 or lose your place. This guide walks the whole path, and is honest about the numbers HACM does not publish.

Updated August 8, 2026 ·9 min read ·By the Rent Finder Cleveland team

Who runs the program: HACM, and how a voucher from elsewhere gets here

In the City of Milwaukee, the Housing Choice Voucher Program belongs to the Housing Authority of the City of Milwaukee (HACM), with its office at 5011 W. Lisbon Avenue, Milwaukee, WI 53210. One detail that changes who actually picks up the phone: HACM announced on January 2, 2025 that it "has contracted with CVR Associates, Inc. to administer the HCV Program, effective January 2, 2025," and that the program "will continue to operate out of its current location (5011 W. Lisbon Ave.) and maintain the same general phone number (414-286-5650)." So the address and the number are unchanged, but the staff working your file are CVR's. HACM describes the mechanics in one sentence worth memorizing before you start:

The rental subsidy, known as a housing assistance payment, is paid directly to the property owner or designated payee each month. This program is available to a limited number of families and allows them to pay approximately 30% of their income toward their monthly rent.

Two things follow from that. The money does not pass through your hands, and the "approximately 30%" is a rule of thumb rather than a promise — the actual figure is calculated, and we walk through the calculation further down.

Milwaukee County is larger than the City of Milwaukee, and other public housing agencies do operate in Wisconsin. We are not going to name authorities we did not verify, so here is the practical rule instead: if you already hold a voucher from any other housing authority anywhere in the country, portability is your route into Milwaukee, and you begin it with the authority that issued your voucher, not with HACM. Federal rule 24 CFR § 982.353(b) is unusually generous on this point — a voucher-holder or participant family "has the right to receive tenant-based voucher assistance… to lease a unit outside the initial PHA jurisdiction, anywhere in the United States, in the jurisdiction of a PHA with a tenant-based program."

One trap in that same rule: under § 982.353(c), if neither the head of household nor the spouse had a legal residence in the issuing authority's jurisdiction when the family first applied, the family has no right to portability for the first 12 months after admission — the issuing authority may allow it, but does not have to.

Income limits for Milwaukee County and how HUD sets them

Here is where we part company with most pages on this topic: we are not publishing a Milwaukee County income limit table, because HACM does not publish one on the pages we could open and HUD's income-limits portal did not return the FY2026 Milwaukee County data to us. A wrong income limit sends people away who would have qualified, which is worse than no number at all.

What is verifiable is the structure. Under 24 CFR § 982.201(a), to be eligible an applicant "must be a 'family;' must be income-eligible… and must be a citizen or a noncitizen who has eligible immigration status." Income eligibility generally means being a very low income family, with narrower routes in for certain low-income families. And there is a targeting rule with real teeth, at § 982.201(b)(2)(i):

Not less than 75 percent of the families admitted to a PHA's HCV program during the PHA fiscal year from the PHA waiting list shall be extremely low income families.

Read that as a planning fact rather than a technicality. Three out of four admissions must come from the lowest income band, which is why households near the top of the eligibility range often wait longer even on an open list. Call HACM at the number on its Housing Choice Voucher page and ask for the current income limit for your exact household size before you conclude you do not qualify.

Preferences that can move you up the list

Every housing authority sets local preferences — categories of applicant it will serve ahead of others — and it does so in its Administrative Plan. HACM does not publish its preference list on the pages we were able to open, so we will not invent one.

What we can tell you is what to ask for, in words that will get a useful answer: "What local preferences does HACM apply to this waiting list, what documentation proves each one, and does claiming a preference change the date my application is ranked from?" Preferences usually have to be claimed and proven at application time, and a preference you qualify for but never documented does nothing for you. If a member of your household has a disability, ask separately about the reasonable accommodation process — that is not a preference, it is a separate legal right.

The application itself: information, documents and common rejections

HACM runs applicant business through the RentCafé portal at myportal.hacm.org. Because HACM does not publish a document checklist on the pages we could open, the useful thing we can do is tell you what the federal rule will eventually require proof of, so you can assemble it now:

  • Household composition. Every person who will live in the unit, with dates of birth. Bedroom size is assigned from household composition, and it drives the payment standard later.
  • Identity and Social Security documentation for each member.
  • Citizenship or eligible immigration status — required by § 982.201(a).
  • Income, from every source, for every member. Wages, self-employment, Social Security, disability, pensions, child support, public assistance.
  • Assets and deductions — these change adjusted income, which is what your payment is calculated from.
  • A reachable address, phone and email. The single most common way applications die is a notice sent to an address the applicant no longer uses.

Keep a copy of everything you submit, with the date. If you move, update the portal the same week.

Checking your place on the waiting list without losing it

Log into myportal.hacm.org and keep your record current. Right now that is not a suggestion — HACM has attached a hard date of August 16, 2026 to confirming continued interest for applicants already on the Public Housing and Project-Based Voucher lists. Housing authorities purge lists this way; it is normal, and it is how long-waiting households lose years of accumulated position over an unread email.

Practical habits that cost nothing: log in once a quarter even when nothing has changed, update your address the day you move, and screenshot the confirmation page whenever you submit anything.

The briefing appointment and what you leave with

When you are selected, federal rule 24 CFR § 982.301 requires the authority to give you an oral briefing covering how the program works, family and owner responsibilities, where you may lease, how portability works, and the advantages of areas that do not have a high concentration of low-income families. The rule also states that the PHA "may not discourage the family from choosing to live anywhere in the PHA jurisdiction, or outside the PHA jurisdiction under portability procedures."

You also leave with a packet, and § 982.301(b) lists fifteen things that must be in it. The ones renters most often fail to use:

  • The term of the voucher, suspensions, and the extension policy — including how to request an extension.
  • How the PHA determines your payment standard and your total tenant payment, and how it determines the maximum rent for an assisted unit.
  • The HUD-required tenancy addendum that must be in your lease.
  • The form you use to request approval of a unit, and how to submit it.
  • PHA subsidy standards, including when exceptions are granted and when they are required as a reasonable accommodation.
  • Equal opportunity information, the Section 504 coordinator's contact, a copy of the housing discrimination complaint form, and how to request a reasonable accommodation.
  • A list of landlords known to the PHA who may be willing to lease to you — and the rule requires that list to cover areas outside of poverty or minority concentration.
  • When you are entitled to an informal hearing, and how to request one.

A word on two of those items, because quoted out of context they can read like advice about where to live. The phrases "the advantages of areas that do not have a high concentration of low-income families" and "areas outside of poverty or minority concentration" are the federal rule's own words describing what the housing authority must cover in your briefing and packet. They are the authority's obligation, not a recommendation from us about which neighborhood to pick. We do not steer anyone toward or away from any area, and 24 CFR § 982.301(a)(2) bars the authority from doing it either: it "may not discourage the family from choosing to live anywhere in the PHA jurisdiction." Where you look is your call.

Your search clock and how to use it well

Under 24 CFR § 982.303, "The initial term of a voucher must be at least 60 calendar days," and the term must be printed on the voucher itself. Three features of that clock are worth knowing:

  • Extensions are discretionary — the PHA may grant one or more, in accordance with its Administrative Plan, by written notice to the family.
  • Extension as an accommodation is not discretionary. If the family needs and requests an extension as a reasonable accommodation for a member with disabilities, "the PHA must extend the voucher term up to the term reasonably required for that purpose."
  • The clock stops while your unit is under review. § 982.303(c) requires the PHA to suspend the term from the date you submit a request for approval of the tenancy until the date it notifies you in writing of the decision. Submitting on a unit does not burn your remaining days.

That last point should change how you search. Waiting to submit until you are "sure" costs you days; submitting on a real, available unit pauses the clock.

From request for tenancy approval to keys: inspection, HAP contract and move-in

Once you and an owner agree, the authority has to sign off before anyone gets paid. 24 CFR § 982.305(a) lists what must be true first: the unit is eligible, it has been inspected and passes Housing Quality Standards, the lease includes the tenancy addendum, the rent to owner is reasonable, and — at initial lease-up, where gross rent exceeds the payment standard — the family share does not exceed 40 percent of monthly adjusted income.

On timing, the regulation splits by agency size. For a PHA with more than 1,250 budgeted vouchers, the inspection and notice must happen "within a reasonable time" and, "[t]o the extent practicable," within fifteen days of the request — and the 15-day clock is suspended during any period when the unit is not available for inspection. Before the lease term starts, the unit must have passed, the lease must be signed with the HUD tenancy addendum and the lead-based paint disclosure information, and the PHA must have approved the leasing.

Then the contract: "The HAP contract must be executed no later than 60 calendar days from the beginning of the lease term," no assistance payment may be made before it is executed, and a contract executed after the 60 days is void absent a HUD-approved extension. Practical translation for a renter: do not accept keys and move in before the inspection has passed and the paperwork is in. Rent that accrues outside an executed contract is rent you may end up owing yourself.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Milwaukee Section 8 waiting list open?
As of August 8, 2026, no. HACM's page states plainly: "The Section 8 tenant-based rent assistance waiting list is currently CLOSED." Two things are still moving, though: HACM says "WE ARE CURRENTLY ACCEPTING PORTABILITY," so a voucher issued by another housing authority can be moved to Milwaukee, and the Public Housing and Project-Based Voucher waiting lists are open. Check HACM's own page before acting — list status changes without much notice.
What is the income limit for Section 8 in Milwaukee County?
We are not going to publish a dollar figure we could not verify. HUD sets income limits by county and family size and updates them annually, and HACM does not publish the Milwaukee County table on the pages we were able to open. What the federal rule does say is the category: to be income-eligible an applicant must generally be a "very low income" family, and not less than 75 percent of the families a PHA admits from its waiting list in a fiscal year must be extremely low income families. Ask HACM for the current table for your household size.
How long is the wait for a housing voucher in Milwaukee?
HACM does not publish an average wait time, so any specific number you see online is someone's guess. What is published is that the tenant-based waiting list is currently closed, and that applicants already on the Public Housing and Project-Based Voucher lists must confirm continued interest by August 16, 2026 to keep their place. Portability is the faster route if you already hold a voucher elsewhere.
What documents do I need to apply for Section 8 in Milwaukee?
HACM does not publish a document checklist on the pages we could open, so treat any list you find online as unofficial. The federal eligibility rule tells you what will have to be proven: that your household is a "family," that it is income-eligible under 24 CFR part 5 subpart F, and that members are citizens or noncitizens with eligible immigration status. In practice that means identity and Social Security documentation for every member, birth dates, and verifiable income for everyone in the household. Get the actual list from HACM.
How do I check my HACM waiting list status?
Through the RentCafé applicant portal at myportal.hacm.org. This is not optional housekeeping right now: HACM states that those currently on the waiting list "must update their information and confirm continued interest by August 16, 2026, through the RentCafé applicant portal at myportal.hacm.org to remain on the waiting list." Missing that confirmation can cost you your place.
