What the 53210 ZIP covers, and why HACM's own address matters here

53210 is on Milwaukee's west side. One fact about it is unusually concrete and worth knowing before you go anywhere else: HACM's office sits inside this ZIP, at 5011 W. Lisbon Avenue, Milwaukee, WI 53210. If you are working a voucher, the agency you will be dealing with is not across town from the houses you are looking at.

If a listing you are looking at carries a neighborhood name instead of a ZIP, we work through why the two do not line up — and what to do about it — on our Sherman Park page. The short version: use the ZIP printed on the listing, because that is the field HACM's payment standard schedule keys on.

HACM's schedule carries a base standard for the Milwaukee metropolitan area plus separate, higher amounts for certain exception ZIP codes. Which ZIPs carry an exception is on HACM's own document, and that document is not retrievable for us to verify, so we do not reproduce the list. When you call HACM, ask both questions: the base standard for your family unit size, and whether the ZIP of the specific address carries an exception standard.

Housing stock: brick bungalows, duplexes and larger four-square homes

What we manage on this side of the city is houses on narrow, deep lots: detached bungalows, two-family houses where you rent the upper or lower flat, and larger two-story homes. Each flat in a two-family has its own entrance, kitchen and bath; the basement, the driveway and sometimes the yard are shared.

We are not going to give you a percentage breakdown of the stock by age or type. The Census data endpoint that would answer that now requires an access key and we did not obtain the data, and we would rather leave a number out than publish one we cannot source. Take the description above as what you will physically encounter, not as a statistic.

The date that carries legal consequences is 1978. Federal rules define "target housing" as "any housing constructed prior to 1978," with narrow exclusions (24 CFR §35.86). For target housing, before you are obligated under a lease the lessor must give you an EPA-approved lead hazard pamphlet, disclose any known lead-based paint or hazards including their location and the condition of the painted surfaces, and hand over any records or reports they have (24 CFR §35.88). Ask for it in writing.

Getting around: MCTS bus routes, Capitol Drive and North Avenue corridors

Bus service is run by the Milwaukee County Transit System (MCTS). The published fares: $2.75 per ride for riders 12 to 64, with fare capping at $8.25 daily, $33 weekly and $99 monthly; $1.25 per ride reduced fare for riders 65 and over, riders 6 to 11, and riders with a qualifying disability; children five and under ride free. Cash is accepted, and the restriction on it is narrower than it is usually reported: MCTS states that transfers and fare capping do not work with cash fares paid onboard the bus, while a ticket bought with cash at a ticket vending machine does include the transfer. Fare capping itself runs through the WisGo card and the Umo app. Over a month, that is the difference between $99 capped and paying $2.75 every boarding.

Renters ask us which routes serve the Capitol Drive and North Avenue corridors. We do not print route numbers on a page like this, on purpose: MCTS revises service on a schedule — its site currently announces fall service changes starting August 23 that update schedules on five routes — and a stale route number sends someone to a stop that is no longer served. Put the exact street address of the house into the MCTS Trip Planner or the Real-Time Bus Tracker, and check it against the hours you actually travel. Weekday midday service and Sunday evening service are not the same thing.

We also do not publish drive times. We have no source for them we would stand behind, and a traffic-dependent app estimate is not a fact about a ZIP code.

Using a HACM voucher in 53210

HACM pays its share, the housing assistance payment, directly to the property owner each month; you pay the balance, which HACM describes as approximately 30% of your income.

The precise version: your total tenant payment is the highest of 30% of adjusted monthly income, 10% of monthly income, the housing-designated portion of welfare assistance, or the minimum rent (24 CFR §5.628). The subsidy is the lower of the payment standard minus that amount, or the gross rent minus that amount. And at initial occupancy, if gross rent exceeds the payment standard, your share cannot exceed 40% of your adjusted monthly income (24 CFR §982.508).

Clearing the payment standard is not the same as being approved. HACM "may not approve a lease until the PHA determines that the initial rent to owner is a reasonable rent," measured against comparable unassisted units on location, quality, size, unit type and age, plus amenities and the utilities the owner supplies (24 CFR §982.507).

As of August 2026, HACM's tenant-based Section 8 waiting list is closed, but HACM states it is currently accepting portability (confirm any of this with HACM directly — its website does not serve pages to us, so we cannot re-check it for you) — a voucher issued by another housing authority can be moved to Milwaukee, and you start that with the issuing authority, not with HACM. Separately, HACM's Public Housing and Project-Based Voucher waiting lists are open, and anyone already on them must confirm continued interest by August 16, 2026 through myportal.hacm.org or lose their place. Full detail on our Milwaukee HACM page and in our voucher program explainer.

Yards, garages and off-street parking on Milwaukee lots

Milwaukee's residential lots are narrow and deep, which is why the garage is usually a detached structure at the back, reached by an alley or a side drive, rather than attached at the front. That layout produces the three questions worth asking at every showing:

  • Is the garage or parking pad included in the rent, or rented separately? Get the answer into the lease.
  • In a two-family, what is shared? Basement, laundry hookups, driveway, yard, trash bins. Ask which are exclusively yours.
  • Who clears snow, and from what? Walk, drive, steps. In Milwaukee this is a real cost and a real liability, and "we'll figure it out" is not an answer.

One protection that comes with a Wisconsin tenancy regardless of what the lease says: a landlord may only enter during the tenancy to inspect, repair or show the unit, and needs at least 12 hours' advance notice unless you agree to less. The exceptions are narrow — your advance consent, a health or safety emergency, or your absence combined with a reasonable belief that entry is needed to protect the property from damage — and the landlord must announce their presence and identify themselves on request.

Heating an older Milwaukee house: what to ask before you sign

This is the question that separates an affordable Milwaukee rental from an expensive one, and it is answerable before you sign.

Ask what the heat is and who pays it. On most of our houses electric and natural gas are in the tenant's name with We Energies. For a voucher tenancy the lease must state exactly which utilities and appliances the owner supplies and which the family supplies (24 CFR §982.308(d)(5)). Tenant-paid utilities are offset by HACM's utility allowance, which federal rule requires the authority to maintain "for all tenant-paid utilities (except telephone)," classified by category — space heating, air conditioning, cooking, water heating, water, sewer, trash collection, other electric, tenant-supplied refrigerator, tenant-supplied range and applicable surcharges — with no allowance for non-essential costs such as cable or satellite television (24 CFR §982.517). We do not republish HACM's dollar figures here: HACM's own document is not retrievable for us to re-verify, and a stale allowance is a wrong rent calculation. Ask HACM for the current schedule for your bedroom count and the exact utility setup in the house — the allowance changes your out-of-pocket rent directly. Our guide to what utilities are included covers the same questions from the Ohio side.

Ask whether the system can actually hold temperature. Wisconsin requires a landlord to disclose, before signing or taking a deposit, heating facilities that are not in safe operating condition or "not capable of maintaining a temperature, in all living areas of the dwelling unit, of at least 67°F (19°C) during all seasons of the year in which the dwelling unit may be occupied." And under HUD's national condition standards, a unit may not contain unvented space heaters that burn gas, oil or kerosene (24 CFR §5.703). A space heater doing a furnace's job is a failure, not a feature.

Know the help that exists. Wisconsin's Home Energy Assistance Program (WHEAP) helps with heating and electric bills. The current program year runs October 1, 2025 through September 30, 2026, eligibility is based on 60% of Wisconsin's median income, and applications go through energybenefit.wi.gov or 1-866-HEATWIS (1-866-432-8947). Assistance is "a one-time payment during the heating season," which runs October 1 to May 15. Apply early: the state says plainly that benefits are not guaranteed even to eligible households, and that once a program year's funds are exhausted no further benefits are issued.

Applying and move-in timeline

Bring photo ID, proof of income and your voucher packet. Wisconsin caps the screening charge: actual cost up to $25 for a consumer credit report, notice before it is pulled, a copy of the report for you, and no charge at all if you supply your own report less than 30 days old (Wis. Stat. §704.085).

Without a voucher, approval to keys is usually fast. With a voucher, two steps set the pace: submitting a request for tenancy approval along with a copy of the lease including the HUD tenancy addendum, during the term of your voucher (24 CFR §982.302(c)), and HACM's inspection, which must happen before the initial lease term begins (24 CFR §982.405(a)). Do not give notice at your current place until the inspection passes. Your voucher's search clock is suspended while that request is pending.

At move-in, Wisconsin requires the landlord to hand you a check-in sheet and give you 7 days to complete and return it (Wis. Stat. §704.08). Fill it out and photograph everything — our move-in inspection checklist walks the rooms in order. At move-out, the deposit must come back within 21 days, less only lawful deductions, with a written statement itemizing each one; nothing may be deducted for normal wear and tear; and if a violation costs you money you may sue directly and recover twice the loss plus costs and reasonable attorney's fees.

Frequently asked questions

How much is rent in 53210 Milwaukee?
Our Milwaukee houses generally run $700 to $1,800 a month. We do not publish a median rent for 53210 because no primary source we could open publishes one, and we do not publish HACM's payment standards either — HACM's schedule is not retrievable for us to verify. Federal rule does fix the band: a basic payment standard runs 90% to 110% of the published Fair Market Rent, which for FY2026 in the Milwaukee-Waukesha area is $1,007–$1,230 for one bedroom, $1,204–$1,471 for two and $1,483–$1,812 for three. Ask HACM for the exact figure for your family unit size.
Where is HACM's office if I rent in 53210?
HACM's office is at 5011 W. Lisbon Avenue, Milwaukee, WI 53210 — inside this ZIP code. As of August 2026 its tenant-based Section 8 waiting list is closed, but HACM states it is currently accepting portability, so a voucher issued by another housing authority can be moved to Milwaukee. You start that with the authority that issued your voucher, not with HACM.
Do landlords in 53210 accept housing vouchers?
Every house we manage in Milwaukee accepts one, and HACM's own office is in this ZIP, at 5011 W. Lisbon Avenue. Whether a private landlord in Wisconsin may refuse a voucher is genuinely unsettled. Wis. Stat. §106.50(1m)(nm) includes lawful source of income among the protected classes, but the statute never defines the term. The Equal Rights Division rule at Wis. Admin. Code §DWD 220.02(8) defines it openly and covers any negotiable draft, coupon or voucher representing monetary value, though its only example is food stamps and it never names Housing Choice Vouchers. Meanwhile the official statute text carries an annotation pointing to Knapp v. Eagle Property Management Corp., 54 F.3d 1272 (1995), summarized as holding that federal rent vouchers are not clearly within the meaning of the term. The state publishes both without reconciling them, so no one can say categorically that a landlord may reject a voucher, or that they may not. For a specific situation, consult a housing attorney licensed in Wisconsin. What is certain is our position: we accept Housing Choice Vouchers on every house we manage.
Do 53210 rentals come with a garage?
Many Milwaukee houses have a detached garage or parking pad at the back of the lot, but whether it is included in your rent is a lease question, not a given. Ask, and get the answer written into the lease along with who clears snow and who has the laundry hookups.
How do I apply for a 53210 rental house?
Book a showing, then apply with photo ID, proof of income and your voucher packet if you have one. Wisconsin caps a credit report charge at the landlord's actual cost up to $25 and requires notice before it is pulled plus a copy for you. With a voucher, HACM must approve the tenancy and inspect the unit before the lease term starts.
