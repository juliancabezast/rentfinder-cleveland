Section 8 Landlords in Maple Heights: Registration First, RFTA Second

Maple Heights is one of 17 Cuyahoga County suburbs where CMHA must collect a Certificate of Occupancy and/or Rental Registration with the Request for Tenancy Approval. The City issues that document, not CMHA, and CMHA's clocks do not start until the packet is submitted — so get it first.

Maple Heights is on CMHA's 17-suburb list

There is no CMHA license to obtain. Its Leasing with CMHA page states that "CMHA does not have a pre-approval process for landlords or units," and its Administrative Plan agrees: "The PHA does not formally approve an owner to participate in the HCV program."

What CMHA does collect in Maple Heights is a document City Hall issues. CMHA must collect a Certificate of Occupancy and/or Rental Registration with the RFTA for units in 17 named suburbs: Bedford, Bedford Heights, Brook Park, Cleveland Heights, Cuyahoga Heights, East Cleveland, Euclid, Fairview Park, Garfield Heights, Lakewood, Lyndhurst, Maple Heights, Parma, Shaker Heights, South Euclid, University Heights and Warrensville Heights.

The operative word is *with*: it rides inside the packet, not as a follow-up weeks later. Cleveland is handled the other way — since October 1, 2025 CMHA "will verify active City of Cleveland rental registrations through the City's Citizen Access Portal." Cleveland owners get checked; Maple Heights owners attach: which suburbs require what.

Fees, renewal cadence and how long the City takes are Maple Heights' to set, and we will not estimate them. Call the City before you advertise.

Why the order matters: which clock starts when

CMHA publishes policy targets, not turnaround statistics, and each is written against an event. The initial inspection, the pass/fail decision and the notice to owner and family come "within 15 days of submission of the Request for Tenancy Approval (RTA)" — suspended for any period the unit is not available. The tenancy determination is separate: "within 10 business days of receiving all required information."

Submission starts one clock. Complete information starts the other. Time spent obtaining a certificate falls before both, so it never shows in CMHA's numbers — and shows in full on your vacancy and the tenant's voucher.

CMHA tells landlords to check the voucher's expiration date, "180 days from the issue date"; the RFTA and proposed lease must be submitted no later than that date. A family runs one packet at a time — "The family may not submit, and the PHA will not process, more than one (1) RTA at a time" — so while you chase paperwork, your unit holds their only slot. CMHA also "will not accept missing information or corrections by phone."

What else rides in the packet, plus a gate added January 1, 2026

The RFTA is not yours to request. CMHA's step 4 is "Submit Request for Tenancy Approval Packet (RFTA) provided to you by HCV Family." It "must be signed by both the family and the owner," and "[t]he owner may submit the RTA on behalf of the family" — to rfta@cmha.net or the kiosks in CMHA's Main Campus lobby, 8120 Kinsman Road. The packet explained.

Two items block execution: no HAP contract "until the owner has submitted IRS form W-9," and "All owners are required to go on direct deposit to receive HAP payments." CMHA also verifies ownership through the County Auditor's website and confirms the unit is not in foreclosure and current on taxes.

Since January 1, 2026, submitting an RFTA triggers a requirement that the owner or designated managing agent complete CMHA's Virtual Landlord Orientation — once every 24 months — before CMHA can execute a HAP contract for a new tenancy. It runs about 90 minutes, live and virtual, booked through Eventbrite. "Existing HAP contracts and current HAP payments are not affected." What changed.

The certificate is asked for again, at the pay window

Most owners file the registration away as intake paperwork. CMHA names it again describing what must land before a first check moves: "three copies of the signed contracts, the executed lease and occupancy permit, when applicable."

Payment dates are "generally on the 3rd and 16th of each month." A certificate that is missing, expired or in the wrong name therefore costs you twice. Handing over keys early costs more: "HCVP will not pay the back amount retroactively."

Nobody in Maple Heights can require you to do this

The U.S. Commission on Civil Rights' Ohio Advisory Committee puts it plainly: "Source-of-income discrimination is legal in most of Ohio." The federal Fair Housing Act "does not prohibit landlords from refusing to accept tenant applications based on a tenant's lawful income source." Five Cuyahoga County municipalities ban it — Cleveland Heights, University Heights, South Euclid, Warrensville Heights and Linndale — and Maple Heights is not among them.

So the case has to be worth-it, made honestly. CMHA's owner benefit list promises "[s]table, guaranteed rent payments deposited directly into your account." That word is CMHA's, not ours: no unit is approved until it passes, no contract exists until it is executed, and "HCVP does not pay for tenant caused damages." What is real is the pool — roughly 15,000 voucher holders in Cuyahoga County, 20,000 households waiting, and CMHA's testimony that only about 50% leased before their voucher expired.

Frequently asked questions

Does Maple Heights need a certificate before CMHA will process my RFTA?
CMHA must collect a Certificate of Occupancy and/or Rental Registration with the Request for Tenancy Approval for units in 17 named suburbs, and Maple Heights is one of them. The City issues it, not CMHA — which has no landlord application or pre-approval process at all.
How much does the registration add to my lease-up timeline?
CMHA publishes no figure, because that time sits outside its clocks. Its targets start at submission of the RFTA — inspect, decide and notify within 15 days — and at receipt of all required information, for a determination within 10 business days. The tenant's 180-day voucher runs throughout.
What happens if something in the packet is missing?
It gets corrected in writing: CMHA reviews the RFTA for completeness and consistency with the proposed lease, and will not accept missing information or corrections by phone. Because a family may run only one Request for Tenancy Approval at a time, a round trip on your file delays their search.
Who submits the RFTA — me or the tenant?
The voucher holder brings you the packet; CMHA's step 4 is to submit the RFTA packet provided to you by the HCV family. It must be signed by both the family and the owner, and CMHA policy lets the owner submit it on the family's behalf, by email to rfta@cmha.net.
Do I have to accept a housing voucher in Maple Heights?
No. Ohio has no statewide source-of-income protection, and the federal Fair Housing Act does not cover a tenant's lawful income source. Five Cuyahoga County municipalities ban source-of-income discrimination — Cleveland Heights, University Heights, South Euclid, Warrensville Heights and Linndale — and Maple Heights is not one of them. There is no county-wide ordinance.
