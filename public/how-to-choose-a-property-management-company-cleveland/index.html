How to Choose a Cleveland Property Manager (and What to Ask First)

Judge a Cleveland manager on deadlines, not promises. Ask first whether they have completed CMHA's Virtual Landlord Orientation, required since January 1, 2026 before a HAP contract can be executed for a new tenancy. Then read the agreement for when fees are charged and who approves repairs.

Start with the credential you can verify in one sentence

Since January 1, 2026, submitting a Request for Tenancy Approval triggers a requirement that the owner or the designated managing agent complete CMHA's Virtual Landlord Orientation — once every 24 months, and before CMHA can execute a HAP contract for a new tenancy. So the first question is not "how many doors do you manage?" but "have you completed CMHA's orientation, and when?"

It is a live virtual session of about 90 minutes booked through Eventbrite, and CMHA verifies attendance before executing the contract. Existing HAP contracts and current payments are unaffected, so the answer also dates their last new voucher tenancy. What changed on January 1, 2026 covers it. We have completed it.

Ask them to name the clocks

A voucher tenancy is a sequence of deadlines with money attached to each; a manager who cannot name them will meet them by luck.

One follow-up separates the experienced from the fluent: what is the difference between withholding and abatement? Money withheld under 24 CFR 982.404(d)(1) comes back once the unit complies; abatement is defined as stopping payments "with no potential for retroactive payment." Paperwork questions that predict how lease-up will go

Most voucher deals do not fail at the inspection. They stall in a packet.

  1. "Will you file the RFTA yourself, and how fast do corrections go back?" CMHA lets the owner submit on the family's behalf — to rfta@cmha.net or the kiosks in its Kinsman Road lobby — takes no corrections by phone, and processes one RFTA per family at a time, so a stalled packet also blocks that household from other units.
  2. "Are the W-9 and direct deposit in, and whose account?" CMHA will not execute the HAP contract until the owner submits Form W-9, all owners must go on direct deposit, and CMHA's forms library carries an Agent/Payee form.
  3. "Do I get portal access?" CMHA's Landlord Portal holds payment information and the payment calendar, an exportable ledger, 1099s, caseworker details, inspection history, and failed inspection items with photos within 72 hours. If they hold the only login, you read your income through them.
  4. "Who screens the applicant?" CMHA is explicit: screening prospective HCV tenants is "the landlord's responsibility ... just as you would any other tenant."

The local filings a national firm most often misses

CMHA collects a Certificate of Occupancy and/or Rental Registration with the RFTA for units in 17 named suburbs: Bedford, Bedford Heights, Brook Park, Cleveland Heights, Cuyahoga Heights, East Cleveland, Euclid, Fairview Park, Garfield Heights, Lakewood, Lyndhurst, Maple Heights, Parma, Shaker Heights, South Euclid, University Heights and Warrensville Heights. Name your city and see whether they know. Since October 1, 2025 CMHA also verifies active City of Cleveland rental registrations through the City's Citizen Access Portal, so a lapsed one stalls the packet.

Two more belong in the agreement. Cleveland's Residents First law requires a local agent based in Cuyahoga County who takes legal responsibility for the property, enforced with $200 civil tickets whose unpaid fines are added to the tax bill. Lead Safe Certification for pre-1978 rentals runs two years. Ask who holds each.

Six lines in the agreement worth arguing about

Price is the easiest thing to compare and the least useful alone; what Cleveland firms actually charge has the published schedules. These six terms decide whether a percentage is a good deal.

  1. When the monthly fee is charged. On rent collected or rent scheduled, and does it run through a vacancy or an abated month? Ours is 5%, only while rented.
  2. When the placement fee is earned. At signature, at listing, or at placement? Ours is one month's rent, only after the tenant is placed.
  3. Whether a voucher tenancy costs extra. Sometimes it does, openly: Progressive Urban Property Management publishes one month's rent for leasing plus $250 more for voucher tenants, and a $100–$150 setup fee.
  4. Repair authority. The approval threshold, whether estimates arrive itemized, and how fast — it must survive CMHA's 24-hour list. Ours: free, itemized, inside 24 hours, nothing started without approval.
  5. Who attends the inspection, and who eats a reinspection fee. Under 24 CFR 982.405(h) an authority may not bill the owner for the initial inspection or the first during occupancy, but may charge a reasonable reinspection fee the owner cannot pass to the family. We attend and report for $100, or you attend free.
  6. Exit. Notice period, what happens to the portal login and the Agent/Payee designation, and who tells CMHA. A buyer must file CMHA's Change of Ownership packet before payments move.

Answers that should end the interview

  • "The rent is guaranteed." CMHA's own page lists "[s]table, guaranteed rent payments deposited directly into your account" — marketing language for the subsidy portion. It is not the tenant's share, it does not survive abatement, and "HCVP does not pay for tenant caused damages." What CMHA pays and what it doesn't.
  • "We'll get you approved with CMHA." There is nothing to approve: "CMHA does not have a pre-approval process for landlords or units," and its FAQ answers the qualification question with "No application process is required."
  • "We can expedite the inspection." CMHA publishes no expedited, fast-track, pre-inspection or self-certification option.
  • "Move them in now, fix the paperwork after." CMHA warns that a tenant let in early owes the full rent, with nothing paid retroactively. A side rent arrangement is worse: CMHA calls side deals illegal, and they can leave a landlord "disbarred, suspended or subject to a limited denial of participation."
  • "Everyone here has to take vouchers" — or "nobody does." Neither holds countywide: five Cuyahoga municipalities ban source-of-income discrimination, and the City of Cleveland does not list source of income among its protected classes.

Frequently asked questions

What should I ask a Cleveland property manager first?
Ask whether they have completed CMHA's Virtual Landlord Orientation, and when. Since January 1, 2026, submitting an RFTA triggers a requirement that the owner or designated managing agent complete it every 24 months, before CMHA can execute a HAP contract for a new tenancy.
Does a property management company need CMHA approval?
No. CMHA states it "does not have a pre-approval process for landlords or units," and its landlord FAQ answers the qualification question with "No application process is required." What is required is the orientation before a new HAP contract is executed, a Form W-9, and direct deposit.
Do Cleveland property managers charge more for a Section 8 tenant?
Some publish a surcharge. Progressive Urban Property Management lists one full month's rent for leasing plus an additional $250 for voucher tenants, and a $100 to $150 per-unit setup fee. Others publish no voucher line at all, which is not the same as no charge. Ask directly.
Which contract terms matter most on a voucher rental?
Whether the monthly fee runs during vacancy or abatement, when the placement fee is earned, whether a voucher tenancy is surcharged, the repair-approval threshold and estimate turnaround, who attends inspections and absorbs a reinspection fee, and the exit clause covering notice, portal access and the Agent/Payee designation.
