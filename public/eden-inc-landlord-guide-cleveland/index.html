What Is EDEN Inc., and How Does a Cleveland Landlord Partner With It?

EDEN, Inc. — Emerald Development and Economic Network — is a Cleveland 501(c)(3) nonprofit that administers supportive-housing rental assistance, separate from CMHA. Becoming an EDEN housing provider takes six steps: review the Welcome Packet, submit a direct deposit form, a Property Owner Disclosure Form and a W-9, list your property, then complete the Request for Lease Approval.

EDEN, Inc. at a glance

EDEN's full legal name is Emerald Development and Economic Network, Inc. It is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit that filed Articles of Incorporation with the Ohio Secretary of State on December 12, 1990 and became operational after its first board meeting on February 26, 1991 — so 2026 marks its 35th year. It began with a single residence in 1991.

Today EDEN operates as a contract agency of the Alcohol, Drug Addiction and Mental Health Services (ADAMHS) Board of Cuyahoga County, and its Executive Director is Elaine Gimmel. Its service area spans Ashtabula, Cuyahoga, Lake and Lorain Counties.

EDEN administers rental assistance for more than 2,200 households every month and is the primary provider of the Rapid Re-Housing Program in Cuyahoga County. In 2025 it served 4,083 households (7,349 individuals) — 10,913 people assisted in total that year. EDEN also owns the Norma Herr Women's Center, the county's primary emergency shelter for single adult women, and owns or manages roughly 850 units of supportive housing with another 112 in development.

EDEN is not CMHA

A frequent point of confusion: EDEN is a separate organization from the Cuyahoga Metropolitan Housing Authority (CMHA). EDEN's own client FAQ tells applicants to *also* contact CMHA to sign up for its voucher and public-housing waitlist — two organizations, two waitlists. EDEN administers its own voucher programs, including a Mainstream Housing Choice Voucher program and a Gateway program, at edencle.org.

EDEN and CMHA are not administrative "partners." The only documented operational overlap is one EDEN supportive-housing building — The Lotus, at 1417 E 45th Street — appearing on CMHA's project-based voucher property list. For who cuts the check under each program, see who pays landlords under CMHA, EDEN and CoC; for EDEN's Mainstream program specifically, our Mainstream voucher landlord guide covers it.

One more thing owners get wrong: you cannot send a prospective tenant to EDEN to "get a voucher." EDEN states that most of its programs do not accept applications directly from the public — the majority of referrals come from approved agencies and systems, and its Coordinated Intake/Entry does not provide referrals for housing, vouchers, or subsidies.

The six steps to become an EDEN housing provider

EDEN's Housing Provider/Manager Welcome Packet lays out a six-step onboarding path. The first five you can complete up front; the sixth happens once a tenant is matched to your unit.

  1. Review EDEN's Housing Provider Welcome Packet — the document that spells out the programs, timelines and owner responsibilities.
  2. Submit a direct deposit form so EDEN can pay its rental-assistance portion electronically.
  3. Submit a Property Owner/Manager Disclosure Form.
  4. Submit a W-9.
  5. List your property with EDEN.
  6. Complete the Request for Lease Approval (RFLA) once a tenant has been identified for your unit.

Documents EDEN requires with the RFLA

The RFLA is the pivot point — nothing moves until EDEN has a complete one. Submit it with:

  • A completed direct deposit form
  • A completed W-9
  • A Certificate of Occupancy or rental registration (required for long-term voucher programs)
  • Documentation from the county auditor's website showing property taxes are current — or a payback agreement if taxes are owed

How the rent and the tenant's share work

EDEN's programs cover six voucher program families, and the tenant's share of rent varies by program: 30% of income on long-term supportive-housing subsidies such as Continuum of Care and H-TBRA, 0% on both Rapid Re-Housing programs (for 12 months), 30% on Re-Entry and the Housing Assistance Program, and 30–40% on the Mainstream Housing Choice Voucher program. Under every EDEN program the tenant pays their portion directly to you — EDEN pays only its subsidy portion.

On the rent ceiling, EDEN's November 2023 Rent Determination Policy states it aims to keep approved rents at or below HUD Fair Market Rent for the jurisdiction.

What happens after you submit the RFLA

Once EDEN has a complete RFLA, the clock starts:

  • Rent determination: within 3–4 business days of EDEN receiving the RFLA.
  • Inspection: the initial inspection is scheduled and completed within 3–10 business days of the RFLA. The unit must pass before move-in and annually thereafter, and EDEN says it cannot enter into a contract with a unit that fails. (EDEN's Welcome Packet describes a Housing Quality Standards (HQS) inspection while its Housing Providers page references a transition to the newer NSPIRE standard — confirm the current standard with EDEN.)
  • Processing: roughly 3 weeks for long-term voucher programs (Continuum of Care, Mainstream, HAP, all Re-Entry, H-TBRA) and roughly 1 week for short-term programs (Rapid Re-Housing, Community Transition Program).
  • HAP contract: for long-term programs, EDEN generates the HAP (Housing Assistance Program) contract once a move-in date is confirmed — Part A covers the unit, utilities, total rent and EDEN's assistance amount; Part B details the terms. You must sign and return it to be paid. (It does not apply to short-term programs.)
  • Payment: EDEN's monthly subsidy arrives between the 1st and 4th of each month. The first payment is sent within 3–4 business days of EDEN receiving the passed inspection report — but no initial payment is made until the signed lease is on file.

Inspections, repairs and fees

If a unit fails inspection, the owner has 30 days to make repairs (24 hours for life-threatening conditions). Assistance payments may be withheld during that window, and the tenant is not responsible for EDEN's portion of the rent while payments are withheld.

A few cost boundaries: EDEN does not cover parking fees, key deposits or regular pet deposits — those are the tenant's — and, by law, you cannot require a pet deposit for a documented assistance animal, because it is not considered a pet.

Frequently asked questions

Is EDEN the same as CMHA?
No. EDEN, Inc. (Emerald Development and Economic Network) is a separate Cleveland 501(c)(3) nonprofit with its own voucher programs and waitlists; CMHA is the Cuyahoga Metropolitan Housing Authority, and EDEN's own FAQ tells applicants to contact CMHA separately. They are not administrative partners — the only documented overlap is one EDEN building on CMHA's project-based voucher list.
What are the six steps to become an EDEN housing provider?
Review EDEN's Housing Provider Welcome Packet, submit a direct deposit form, submit a Property Owner/Manager Disclosure Form, submit a W-9, list your property with EDEN, and complete the Request for Lease Approval (RFLA) once a tenant is identified. The first five steps can be done up front; the RFLA comes when a tenant is matched.
What documents does EDEN's RFLA require?
A completed direct deposit form, a completed W-9, a Certificate of Occupancy or rental registration (for long-term voucher programs), and documentation from the county auditor showing property taxes are current — or a payback agreement if taxes are owed. EDEN will not proceed until the package is complete.
How long until EDEN pays after I sign up?
After a complete RFLA, rent determination takes 3–4 business days and the inspection is done within 3–10 business days. Full processing runs about 3 weeks for long-term programs and about 1 week for short-term ones. Monthly payments arrive between the 1st and 4th, but the first payment waits for a passed inspection and a signed lease.
Can I send a tenant to EDEN to get a voucher?
No. EDEN says most of its programs do not accept applications directly from the public; the majority of referrals come from approved agencies and systems, and its Coordinated Intake/Entry does not provide referrals for housing, vouchers, or subsidies. Tenants reach EDEN through that referral system, not a landlord.
Who do I contact at EDEN to get started?
Reach EDEN through its Property Owner Information Line at 216-503-6369 or EDENLandlordInfo@EDENcle.org; the main line is 216-961-9690 and the office is at 7812 Madison Avenue, Cleveland, OH 44102. EDEN advertises housing-provider incentives in its newsletters but publishes no dollar amounts, so ask what is currently available.
