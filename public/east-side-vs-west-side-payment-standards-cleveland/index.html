East Side vs. West Side: How CMHA Payment Standards Compare Across Cleveland

CMHA sets Section 8 payment standards by ZIP code, not by a single east–west line. For FY2026, the near–West Side ZIP 44113 (Ohio City/Tremont) tops the county at $2,169 for a two-bedroom, but eastern Cleveland Heights (44118) runs above the county median while Slavic Village (44127) anchors the bottom at $1,096. The short answer: the ceiling follows your ZIP, not a straight east–west line

CMHA sets Housing Choice Voucher payment standards by ZIP code, not as one countywide number. Under Small Area Fair Market Rents (SAFMRs), the agency publishes a separate ceiling for each of the 53 ZIP codes in its service area, and those FY2026 figures took effect January 1, 2026. So the honest answer to "is the west side higher than the east side?" is: sometimes — but the real driver is your ZIP, not the side of town.

The single highest tier does sit on the near West Side, in ZIP 44113 (Ohio City / Tremont). But high ceilings are not a West Side monopoly. The eastern inner-ring suburb of Cleveland Heights (ZIP 44118) carries one of the higher two-bedroom standards in the county, while the lowest ceilings sit on Cleveland's southeast side in ZIPs like 44127 (Slavic Village). Where your property lands in that spread depends almost entirely on its ZIP.

This is not a CMHA quirk but a federal requirement: CMHA (HUD code OH003) is on HUD's list of housing authorities required to use Small Area FMRs, with a designation year of 2024, and it began applying SAFMR payment standards on January 1, 2025. For how the mechanism works, see what SAFMR means for Cleveland landlords.

Reading the spread

For a two-bedroom, the ceiling nearly doubles — from $1,096 in ZIP 44127 to $2,169 in ZIP 44113 — a spread of roughly 98%. That is exactly why any single "CMHA payment standard" quoted without a ZIP code is misleading: the county median two-bedroom is $1,525, but very few ZIPs actually sit at that median. Note also that the top tier is shared by two ZIPs, 44113 and 44040 — so even "the highest ceiling" is not an exclusively West Side story.

What the four ZIPs tell you

Two things stand out. First, the near–West Side ZIP 44113 does top this group — its two-bedroom ceiling is more than a thousand dollars higher than Slavic Village's. Second, the east side is not uniformly low: Cleveland Heights (44118) clears $1,683 for a two-bedroom, well above the $1,525 county median. The lowest numbers here belong to the southeast-side ZIPs 44105 and 44127.

In other words, "east versus west" is a rough proxy at best. Because the SAFMR ceiling is calculated from each ZIP's own local market data, a property's ZIP — not simply its side of the Cuyahoga River — determines the ceiling, and two neighboring ZIPs can carry very different standards.

Why CMHA prices this way — and what the ceiling actually means

CMHA sets each ZIP's ceiling from HUD's Small Area Fair Market Rent for that ZIP; its policy language says standards are established "between 90% and 110% of the FMRs or approved exceptions as applicable." CMHA describes the SAFMR switch as intended to give families "more access to low-poverty, high-opportunity areas." The practical effect for owners: a stronger-market ZIP can support a materially higher subsidized rent than one blended countywide figure ever would.

One point CMHA is emphatic about: the payment standard is not a rent cap. In its words, "The payment standard is NOT the maximum amount that the landlord can charge; it is the maximum amount of subsidy that the CMHA will pay toward each tenant's rent portion." You may set an asking rent above the standard — subject to CMHA's rent-reasonableness review and the tenant's share limits. The standard tells you how much of your rent the voucher is likely to underwrite, not what you are allowed to ask.

Find where your property lands

There is no static CMHA PDF of payment standards by ZIP. The only current, authoritative source is CMHA's interactive Rent Determination tool, which returns the FY2026 figures for any ZIP you enter. One trap to avoid: the table the tool shows *before* you select a ZIP is a placeholder that matches only ZIPs 44103 and 44105, not a countywide average. Never treat that default row as "the" payment standard; enter your exact ZIP instead.

Frequently asked questions

Is the west side of Cleveland always higher than the east side for Section 8?
No. CMHA sets payment standards by ZIP under Small Area FMRs, so ceilings track each ZIP's local market rather than a single east–west line. The near–West Side ZIP 44113 tops the county, but the eastern suburb of Cleveland Heights (44118) also carries a high two-bedroom standard, while the lowest ceilings sit on the southeast side.
Which Cleveland ZIP has the highest CMHA payment standard, and which the lowest?
For FY2026, ZIP 44113 (Ohio City / Tremont) shares the top tier with ZIP 44040 — up to $2,169 for a two-bedroom and $2,983 for a four-bedroom. ZIP 44127 (Slavic Village area) carries the lowest, at $1,096 for a two-bedroom and $1,514 for a four-bedroom. Both are effective January 1, 2026.
Does the payment standard mean I can only charge that amount of rent?
No. CMHA states the payment standard "is NOT the maximum amount that the landlord can charge; it is the maximum amount of subsidy that the CMHA will pay toward each tenant's rent portion." You can ask above it, subject to CMHA's rent-reasonableness review and the tenant's share limits. The standard governs the subsidy, not your asking price.
Why do two neighboring ZIP codes have different payment standards?
Because Small Area Fair Market Rents are calculated per ZIP from that ZIP's local market data. CMHA (HUD code OH003) is required to use SAFMRs, so adjacent ZIPs can carry noticeably different ceilings — the county's two-bedroom standard ranges from $1,096 to $2,169, a spread of roughly 98%.
How do I find the payment standard for my exact ZIP code?
Use CMHA's interactive Rent Determination tool and enter your ZIP; it returns FY2026 figures by bedroom size. Do not rely on the default table shown before you pick a ZIP — that placeholder matches only ZIPs 44103 and 44105, not a countywide figure. Enter your specific ZIP to see your ceiling.
