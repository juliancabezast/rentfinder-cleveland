Documents You Need to Rent a House in Cleveland: The Full Checklist

For a Cleveland rental you generally need four things: a government photo ID for every adult on the lease, proof of income covering about the last month, your rental history with a working phone number for each past landlord, and — if you have one — your Housing Choice Voucher plus the Request for Tenancy Approval. None of that list is set by Ohio statute; it is what screening actually requires. Assemble it once, keep it in one folder on your phone, and you can apply the same day you tour instead of three days later.

The core packet every Cleveland landlord asks for

Start here. This is the packet that clears the widest range of Cleveland-area landlords, from a five-house owner in Old Brooklyn to a management company in Lakewood:

  • Photo ID — one per adult occupant who will sign.
  • Income proof — the most recent two to four pay stubs, or the substitutes covered below.
  • Rental history — last two addresses, dates in and out, landlord name and phone.
  • Contact details that work — a phone that takes voicemail and an email you actually check. More Cleveland applications stall on an unanswered phone than on a credit score.
  • Voucher packet, if applicable — voucher and Request for Tenancy Approval.

Two habits that shorten everything: scan, do not photograph at an angle — a free scanner app produces a flat PDF a screening portal will accept, where a shadowed phone photo often gets bounced back. And send it as one message. An application arriving in six texts across two days is not an application; it is a to-do list for the person reviewing it.

Photo ID, Social Security number and ITIN: what is actually required

Any current government photo ID normally satisfies the identity step: an Ohio driver license or state ID, a passport, a military ID, or a consular ID depending on the landlord policy.

The Social Security number question deserves a straight answer. We could not find any Ohio statute that requires a renter to have an SSN to sign a lease. What drives the request is screening mechanics: a tenant screening company generally uses an SSN to match you to a credit file. Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act, a landlord may obtain a consumer report on an applicant because they have a legitimate business need for the information ... in connection with a business transaction that is initiated by the consumer (15 U.S.C. 1681b). Some landlords accept an ITIN plus additional identity and income documents; others do not. Ask before you pay any fee, not after.

The voucher program is a separate matter with its own federal rule. To be eligible for a Housing Choice Voucher, an applicant must be a family; must be income-eligible ... and must be a citizen or a noncitizen who has eligible immigration status (24 CFR 982.201). CMHA preliminary application asks for Social Security numbers as part of that process. Do not confuse the two tracks: the housing authority determines who qualifies for the subsidy; the landlord screens who gets the house.

Proving income: pay stubs, offer letters, benefit award letters, bank statements

What a landlord is testing is not really the number. It is whether the number is verifiable, current, and likely to repeat. Pay stubs win because they answer all three at once. When you do not have them, replace each quality deliberately:

  • New job, no stubs yet — a signed offer letter on letterhead with start date, rate, and hours, plus a supervisor phone number.
  • Self-employed or 1099 — last year tax return with the Schedule C, plus three months of bank statements showing deposits.
  • Benefit income — an official award letter from the issuing agency. An award letter is usually stronger than a bank statement, because it states the amount and that it continues.
  • Cash pay — an employer letter stating role, rate, hours, and how long you have worked there, with a number that will be answered when called.

The full playbook for each of these, including a letter you can hand your employer, is in proving income without pay stubs in Ohio. For how much income a Cleveland landlord typically looks for, see income needed to rent a house in Cleveland and the 3x rent rule explained — and note that the 3x standard is a market convention, not an Ohio legal requirement.

Rental history: past addresses, landlord contacts, and what to do if you have none

Write down two addresses, the month you moved in and out of each, and a phone number for the landlord. Then do the step almost nobody does: call the number yourself first. Cleveland has a lot of small owners, and disconnected numbers on old rentals are common. A dead reference number costs you two days while someone tries it, gives up, and moves to the next applicant.

If your last landlord was a relative, say so plainly rather than presenting it as an arm-length tenancy. If you rented a room without a written lease, offer what you do have — a utility bill in your name at that address, cancelled checks, a rent receipt book.

No rental history at all is a normal starting position, not a disqualification. Substitute stability from another direction: a longer employment record, proof of where you have been living, or a co-signer. What hurts is a blank field, because blanks read as something hidden. And if a Cleveland eviction filing exists in your past, know that under the FCRA, civil suits, civil judgments, and records of arrest generally come off a consumer report after seven years or until the governing statute of limitations has expired, whichever is the longer period (15 U.S.C. 1681c). It ages out; volunteering the context beats being surprised by it.

Extra documents if you hold a CMHA voucher: voucher, briefing packet and the RFTA

Voucher holders run two paperwork tracks at once — the landlord one and the housing authority one — and the delay usually lives in the second.

For the landlord: your voucher, the Request for Tenancy Approval, and the same ID and income documents anyone else brings. Yes, income documents too — the voucher does not replace them.

For CMHA: its preliminary application asks for an active private email account, Social Security numbers, and the names, dates of birth and income of every household member, and the preliminary application stays valid for 18 months (cmha.net). CMHA also publishes household income limits — effective April 3, 2025, ranging from $34,800 for a one-person household to $65,650 for eight people. Federally, not less than 75 percent of families admitted from a waiting list in a PHA fiscal year must be extremely low income (24 CFR 982.201), which is why the waitlist math looks the way it does.

The document-by-document breakdown lives in Section 8 application documents in Cleveland. One number worth carrying in your head: at initial occupancy, when gross rent exceeds the payment standard, the family share must not exceed 40 percent of the family adjusted monthly income (24 CFR 982.508).

Roommates, co-signers and guarantors: who submits what

Roommates. Every adult who will live there submits a full packet — ID, income, rental history — and every adult signs. Half-submitted roommate applications are the single most common reason a group application sits.

Co-signers. A co-signer submits ID and income proof and signs the lease or an addendum, taking on the rent obligation without living there. Landlords who accept them often want a stronger income showing from the co-signer than from an occupant, because the co-signer is paying two housing costs. Details in using a co-signer to rent in Ohio.

Guarantor services. Third-party companies that guarantee your rent for a fee. Ohio does not require any landlord to accept one, so confirm acceptance before you pay.

Whichever route you take, ask one question in writing: is the obligation joint and several? Under joint-and-several liability, each signer can be pursued for the entire rent, not their share. Better to learn that at signing than at move-out.

What a landlord may not ask you for under the Fair Housing Act

The rule is less about forbidden words than about different treatment. Federal regulation makes it unlawful to use different qualification criteria or applications, or sale or rental standards or procedures, such as income standards, application requirements, application fees, credit analysis or sale or rental approval procedures because of race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status, or national origin (24 CFR 100.60). Under Ohio law, the protected classes in housing are race, color, religion, sex, military status, familial status, ancestry, disability, or national origin (ORC 4112.02).

So the warning sign is not a question — it is a moving target. If you are asked for a document, a fee, or an income multiple that another applicant was not asked for, that is the thing worth writing down, with the date.

One document category that runs the other way: disability-related accommodations. It is unlawful to refuse to make reasonable accommodations in rules, policies, practices, or services, when such accommodations may be necessary to afford a handicapped person equal opportunity to use and enjoy a dwelling (24 CFR 100.204) — the classic example being an assistance animal despite a no-pets policy. Requesting an accommodation is a normal part of an application, not a strike against it. A HUD complaint can be filed not later than one year after the practice occurred or ended (42 U.S.C. 3610).

Application fees in Ohio: what is normal and what you get for it

Here is where most renter guides invent a number. We could not find any Ohio statute or Cleveland ordinance that caps a rental application fee. Ohio Revised Code Chapter 5321 regulates the security deposit — interest and return — not application fees. So the amount is set by each landlord, and the honest advice is to ask two questions before paying: what does the fee cover, and is it refundable if the house is rented to someone else first.

Wisconsin, where we also lease in Milwaukee, is the useful contrast: a landlord there may charge the landlord actual cost, up to $25, to obtain a consumer credit report, and may not charge you at all if you supply your own report from a nationwide agency that is less than 30 days old (Wis. Stat. 704.085). Ohio has no equivalent that we could locate.

What the fee buys you is protection either way. If you are denied based on a consumer report, the FCRA requires oral, written, or electronic notice of the adverse action, identifying the reporting agency and stating that the consumer reporting agency did not make the decision (15 U.S.C. 1681m). You then have the right to a free copy of that report within 60 days and to dispute what is in it. See credit score to rent a house in Cleveland and rental application fees in Ohio.

Print-and-check list: assemble the packet before you tour

  1. Photo ID scanned as PDF — one per adult signer.
  2. Two to four recent pay stubs, or offer letter / award letter / tax return + three months of bank statements.
  3. Two past addresses with dates, landlord names, and phone numbers you have personally tested.
  4. Employer name, supervisor phone, and start date.
  5. Housing Choice Voucher and Request for Tenancy Approval, if you hold one.
  6. A phone number with voicemail set up and an email you check daily.
  7. Everything in one folder, named with your last name, ready to send in one message.

Then book the tour. When you get there and it is the house, you are already done with the hard part — see the Cleveland rental application process for what happens next.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a Social Security number to rent a house in Ohio?
There is no Ohio statute we could find that requires a tenant to have a Social Security number to sign a lease. In practice most screening companies use an SSN to pull a credit file, and some landlords accept an ITIN plus other identity and income documents instead. The voucher program is a separate question: to be eligible for a Housing Choice Voucher an applicant must be a citizen or a noncitizen with eligible immigration status under 24 CFR 982.201, and CMHA asks for Social Security numbers on its preliminary application.
How many pay stubs do Cleveland landlords usually ask for?
Most ask for the most recent two to four, enough to cover about a month of pay. No Ohio law sets that number, so it is landlord policy and it varies. If your pay is irregular, offer more months rather than fewer — a longer record explains a slow week better than an argument does.
Can I rent a house in Cleveland without a bank account?
Yes. A bank account is not a legal requirement to rent in Ohio. Without one you lose bank statements as an income document, so lean on pay stubs, an employer letter, or an official benefit award letter instead — and ask the landlord which payment methods they accept before you sign, since some accept only electronic payment.
What if I have never rented before and have no landlord reference?
Say so up front rather than leaving the field blank. First-time renters typically substitute proof of where they have been living, a steady income record, and a co-signer if the landlord accepts one. A blank rental history reads as evasive; an explained one does not.
Do Section 8 voucher holders need different documents?
You need everything a non-voucher applicant needs, plus your voucher and the Request for Tenancy Approval the housing authority gave you. CMHA runs its own eligibility paperwork separately from the landlord application; its preliminary application asks for an active private email account, Social Security numbers, and the names, dates of birth and income of every household member, and stays valid for 18 months.
