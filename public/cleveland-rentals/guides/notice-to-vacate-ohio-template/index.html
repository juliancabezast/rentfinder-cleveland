How Much Notice to Move Out in Ohio (With a Template You Can Copy)

On a month-to-month tenancy, Ohio Revised Code 5321.17(B) lets either side end it by notice given "at least thirty days prior to the periodic rental date." Week-to-week takes seven days under 5321.17(A). The statute does not prescribe a form, but ORC 5321.16(B) requires your forwarding address in writing — so send one signed letter that does both, and keep proof you delivered it.

Copy-paste notice to vacate template

Start here, because this is the part that actually has to leave your hands. Replace everything in brackets, keep a signed copy for yourself before you send it, and read the delivery and deposit sections below before you pick the termination date. If what you need first is the day count rather than the letter, our month-to-month lease guide works through the notice periods in detail.

[Your name(s)] [Current street address, unit] [City], OH [ZIP] [Phone] · [Email] [Date] [Landlord or property manager name] [Landlord mailing address] RE: 30-Day Notice of Termination of Month-to-Month Tenancy [Property address, unit] Dear [Landlord name]: This letter is written notice, given under Ohio Revised Code section 5321.17(B), that I/we are terminating the month-to-month tenancy at [property address, unit]. The tenancy will terminate on [termination date], which is a periodic rental date and is at least thirty days after the date of this notice. I/we will deliver possession and return all keys, garage remotes and building fobs on or before that date. As required by Ohio Revised Code section 5321.16(B), my/our forwarding address for the itemized written notice and any amount due from the security deposit is: [New street address, unit] [City], [State] [ZIP] Please send the itemized statement and any refund due to that address. [Optional: I/we have occupied the premises for six months or more and request the interest provided under ORC 5321.16(A).] I/we would like to schedule a walkthrough with you before the termination date. Please reply with a date and time that works. Sincerely, _______________________________ Date: __________ [Tenant name, printed] _______________________________ Date: __________ [Tenant name, printed]

The optional interest sentence is worth leaving in if it applies. Under ORC 5321.16(A), a deposit "in excess of fifty dollars or one month's periodic rent, whichever is greater" earns 5% per year on the excess when the tenant stays six months or more. On most Cleveland-area rentals the deposit is at or under one month's rent, so nothing is owed — but if yours was larger, asking in writing costs nothing.

How much notice Ohio actually requires: month-to-month, week-to-week and fixed term

Ohio puts the answer in one short statute. ORC 5321.17 says that "the landlord or the tenant may terminate or fail to renew a month-to-month tenancy by notice given the other at least thirty days prior to the periodic rental date," and that a week-to-week tenancy takes "at least seven days prior to the termination date specified in the notice."

In one line: 30 days counted back from the periodic rental date for month-to-month, 7 days counted back from the termination date you name for week-to-week, and for a fixed term with a stated end date, whatever the lease document says — 5321.17 does not reach it. Note that the rule is symmetrical: the same 30 days you owe your landlord is what the landlord owes you. If it is the day count itself you came for rather than the letter, our month-to-month lease guide is the fuller treatment; the rest of this page is about writing and delivering the notice.

Division (D) of the same section adds a limit worth knowing: 5321.17 "does not apply to a termination based on the breach of a condition of a rental agreement or the breach of a duty and obligation imposed by law, except that it does apply to a breach of the obligation imposed upon a tenant by division (A)(9) of section 5321.05 of the Revised Code." So this is not the section you use when you are leaving because the furnace has been out for three weeks — that route runs through ORC 5321.07, covered further down. This is the section for an ordinary, no-fault move.

Cleveland, East Cleveland, Akron, Lorain and Elyria are all governed by the same state chapter, so the 30-day rule does not change between them. Milwaukee renters are under Wisconsin law instead, which runs on different numbers — see the deposit section below.

Counting the days correctly so your notice lands on time

Here is the trap. "Thirty days prior to the periodic rental date" does not mean thirty days from the day you hand over the letter. It means your termination has to sit at least thirty days after the notice and land on a rental date.

Work an example with rent due the 1st. You decide on June 5 that you want out. June 5 to July 1 is twenty-six days — short. So July 1 is not available to you, and the earliest rental date that clears thirty days is August 1. Had you delivered the same letter on May 30, July 1 would have worked and you would have saved a month of rent. That is what the one-week difference in when you sit down to write is worth: a full extra month of rent, whatever your rent happens to be.

  • Rent due the 1st? Deliver on or before the 1st of the preceding month and you are safe with room to spare.
  • Rent due the 15th? Your rental date is the 15th, not the 1st — count back from the 15th.
  • Week-to-week? You choose the termination date, then make sure the notice is at least seven days ahead of it.

The statute says "at least," so more notice is always safe. Forty-five or sixty days never invalidates a notice; twenty-eight days does. If you are close to the line, name the later rental date. You can always move out early — what you cannot do is retroactively shorten a notice you already sent.

What your notice must contain — including the forwarding address that protects your deposit

ORC 5321.17 says only "notice given the other." It does not require writing, a particular form, or a delivery method. That sounds permissive, and it is exactly why people lose money.

The reason to write it down comes from a different statute. ORC 5321.16(B) states: "The tenant shall provide the landlord in writing with a forwarding address or new address to which the written notice and amount due from the landlord may be sent." And then it puts teeth on it — "If the tenant fails to provide the landlord with the forwarding or new address as required, the tenant shall not be entitled to damages or attorneys fees under division (C) of this section."

Division (C) is the leverage you are protecting. It lets a tenant whose deposit is wrongfully withheld "recover the property and money due him, together with damages in an amount equal to the amount wrongfully withheld, and reasonable attorneys fees." Skip the written forwarding address and you throw away the double-damages remedy before the dispute even starts. Our guides on Ohio security deposit law and getting your deposit back go deeper on that remedy.

So one letter, doing both jobs. Include:

  1. The full property address including unit number — a landlord with twelve doors on the same street needs the unit.
  2. Every adult named on the lease. One roommate's signature does not terminate a joint tenancy.
  3. The termination date, stated as a calendar date, not "in 30 days."
  4. A line identifying it as notice under ORC 5321.17(B) (or 5321.17(A) for week-to-week).
  5. Your forwarding address in writing, plus phone and email.
  6. Date and signature.

How to deliver it and how to prove you did

Because the statute is silent on method, delivery becomes an evidence problem rather than a legal one. If a dispute ever reaches Cleveland Municipal Court, the question will not be "was certified mail required" — it will be "can you show the landlord received it, and when."

  • Certified mail, return receipt requested. The strongest paper trail. Remember the clock is not the postmark in your favor — mail a few days early.
  • Hand delivery. Bring a second copy, ask for a signature and date on it, and photograph the signed copy immediately.
  • Resident portal or email. Fine if your lease allows notice that way, and it timestamps itself. Send it to yourself as well so the message exists outside the landlord's system.
  • Both belt and suspenders. Email it the day you mail it and reference the mailing in the email body.

One thing changes the moment you give notice: your landlord will want to show the home to the next renter. They still have to give you reasonable notice under ORC 5321.04, which says outright that "twenty-four hours is presumed to be a reasonable notice in the absence of evidence to the contrary." Giving notice does not turn your last month into an open house. See the 24-hour entry rule for how that works in practice.

Leaving before the lease ends: what changes and what it costs

If you are inside a fixed term, 5321.17 is not your route — and neither is a 30-day letter. Ohio does not set a statutory early-termination fee, so whatever you owe comes from the lease you signed. Read the early-termination clause first, then read our guide to breaking a lease in Ohio.

There is one statutory exit, and it is narrow. ORC 5321.07 lets a tenant act when the landlord has failed a duty under 5321.04 — the obligation to "make all repairs and do whatever is reasonably necessary to put and keep the premises in a fit and habitable condition," and to "supply running water, reasonable amounts of hot water, and reasonable heat at all times." The sequence is strict: notify the landlord in writing, then wait for the landlord to fail to remedy the condition "within a reasonable time considering the severity of the condition and the time necessary to remedy it, or within thirty days, whichever is sooner." Only then do the three remedies open up — depositing rent with the clerk of the municipal or county court, asking a court for an order, or terminating the rental agreement.

Note the phrase "whichever is sooner." A no-heat January in Cleveland is not a thirty-day problem, and the statute is written to reflect that. Note also what the first remedy is: you deposit the rent with the court clerk. That is not the same as not paying, and treating it as the same is the fastest way to convert a repair dispute into an eviction. Our guides on tenant repair rights and rent escrow walk through the filing.

Whatever the disagreement, ORC 5321.15 forbids self-help: no landlord may initiate "termination of utilities or services, exclusion from the premises, or threat of any unlawful act" to recover possession, and no landlord may "seize the furnishings or possessions of a tenant" for unpaid rent without a court order. And keep the numbers straight — a landlord's three-day notice under ORC 1923.04 is the first step of an eviction case, not a shorter version of your 30-day notice. If you have received one, read the Ohio eviction timeline, not this page.

Move-out inspection, cleaning standard and the 30-day deposit clock

The deposit clock has two triggers, and most renters only know one. ORC 5321.16(B) gives the landlord thirty days to send an itemized written notice with any amount due — but those thirty days run "after termination of the rental agreement and delivery of possession." Holding on to a key for two extra weeks holds the clock open for two extra weeks. Return everything, in one handoff, and note the date.

What can come out of the deposit is also defined: past-due rent, and "the amount of damages that the landlord has suffered by reason of the tenant's noncompliance with section 5321.05 of the Revised Code or the rental agreement." ORC 5321.05 asks you to "keep that part of the premises that he occupies and uses safe and sanitary" and to operate the plumbing and electrical fixtures properly. That is the standard — safe and sanitary. It is not "professionally cleaned," and it is not "as it was on day one." Whatever a deduction is for, it "shall be itemized and identified by the landlord in a written notice delivered to the tenant together with the amount due." A lump sum with no itemization is not what the statute describes.

Ohio's deposit statute never uses the phrase "normal wear and tear," which is why Ohio disputes get argued around 5321.05 instead. Milwaukee renters have it spelled out. Wis. Stat. 704.28 says a landlord must return the deposit "within 21 days" and that the section "does not authorize a landlord to withhold any amount from a security deposit for normal wear and tear." Different state, different clock, different vocabulary — see our breakdown of wear and tear versus damage.

Nothing in 5321.16 sets up a joint move-out walkthrough or gives you a right to attend one, so build your own record instead of relying on the landlord's. On the day you hand over keys, photograph every room empty and well lit, open the oven and the fridge and shoot those too, capture every utility meter reading, and email the whole set to yourself so the timestamps live somewhere neutral. If you are on a month-to-month arrangement and want the mechanics of the tenancy itself, our month-to-month lease guide covers renewal and rent-change rules.

Moving out of a Section 8 unit: telling CMHA and keeping your voucher

With a Housing Choice Voucher, a 30-day letter to your landlord is only half the job. 24 CFR 982.354(d) is explicit: "If the family wants to move to a new unit, the family must notify the PHA and the owner before moving from the old unit." Two notices — one to the owner, one to CMHA in Cuyahoga County (HACM if you are in Milwaukee). And if you are moving outside your PHA's jurisdiction, the same paragraph requires the notice to the initial PHA to "specify the area where the family wants to move."

Do the PHA notice first, before you commit to a termination date, because 982.354(c)(2) lets a PHA adopt policies that "prohibit any move by the family during the initial lease term" and that prohibit "more than one move by the family during any one-year period." A 30-day notice that is valid under Ohio law can still leave you without assistance if the move is not permitted under your PHA's policy. Section 982.354(e)(1) also lets a PHA deny permission to move "if the PHA does not have sufficient funding for continued assistance."

The good news is that 982.354(b)(3) lists "the tenant has given notice of lease termination (if the tenant has a right to terminate the lease on notice to the owner, for owner breach, or otherwise)" as a basis for moving with continued assistance. The exact form, packet and internal deadline are set by each housing authority's own policy rather than by the federal rule, and CMHA does not publish those timelines on its public landlord and applicant pages — so call your caseworker and get the answer in writing before you date the letter.

When you line up the next place, two CMHA facts save time: the payment standard "is NOT the maximum amount that the landlord can charge; it is the maximum amount of subsidy," and since January 1, 2025 CMHA applies Small Area Fair Market Rent payment standards, so the number varies by ZIP code within the county. And at initial occupancy of a new unit, 24 CFR 982.508 caps your share: where gross rent exceeds the payment standard, "the family share must not exceed 40 percent of the family's adjusted monthly income." Frequently asked questions

How many days notice do I have to give my landlord in Ohio?
Under ORC 5321.17(B), either side may end a month-to-month tenancy "by notice given the other at least thirty days prior to the periodic rental date." A week-to-week tenancy takes at least seven days under 5321.17(A), measured against the termination date named in the notice. The rule is identical for the landlord and the tenant. For the full treatment of notice periods and how a month-to-month tenancy works, see our month-to-month lease guide; this page is for writing and delivering the letter.
Does my notice have to be in writing in Ohio?
ORC 5321.17 says only "notice given the other" and does not spell out a form or delivery method. But ORC 5321.16(B) separately requires the tenant to give the landlord a forwarding address in writing, and a tenant who fails to do so is not entitled to damages or attorney fees under 5321.16(C). Since you need a written forwarding address anyway, put both in one signed letter and keep a copy.
What happens if I move out without giving notice?
ORC 5321.17 is the mechanism for terminating a periodic tenancy, and it does not set a penalty for skipping the notice. What it does mean is that the tenancy was not terminated the way the statute contemplates, so any money question is governed by your rental agreement and by ORC 5321.16(B), which lets a landlord apply the deposit to past-due rent and to damages from noncompliance with the agreement. Check your lease and get advice before walking away without notice.
Do I have to give notice if my lease has a fixed end date?
ORC 5321.17 addresses week-to-week and month-to-month tenancies, not the ordinary expiration of a fixed term. If your lease has a stated end date, any notice-of-non-renewal obligation comes from the lease document itself, so read it. Many Cleveland-area leases also convert to month-to-month at the end of the term, and once that happens the 30-day rule in 5321.17(B) applies.
How do I make sure I get my security deposit back?
Give a written forwarding address, hand back possession promptly because the 30-day clock in ORC 5321.16(B) runs from termination of the rental agreement and delivery of possession, and photograph every room on the day you leave. If the landlord wrongfully withholds, 5321.16(C) lets you recover what is owed plus damages equal to the amount wrongfully withheld plus reasonable attorney fees. If you stayed six months or more, ask about the 5% annual interest on any deposit above $50 or one month's rent, whichever is greater.
