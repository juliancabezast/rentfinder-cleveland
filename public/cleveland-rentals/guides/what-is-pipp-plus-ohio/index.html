What Is PIPP Plus and Do You Qualify? Ohio Utility Bills Explained

PIPP Plus replaces your gas and electric bill with a payment based on income: 5% of gross household income for gas plus 5% for electric if you heat with gas, or a flat 10% if you heat with electricity, with a $10.00 monthly minimum. Ohio subsidizes the rest. You qualify at or below 175% of the federal poverty guidelines and only if your utility is regulated by PUCO — which rules out Cleveland Public Power. Twenty-four on-time, in-full payments erase your entire past-due balance.

Updated August 8, 2026 ·7 min read ·By the Rent Finder Cleveland team

What PIPP Plus is and which utilities participate

Most utility help in Ohio is a one-shot credit. HEAP lands once a year; Winter Crisis lands when something is already broken. PIPP Plus — the Percentage of Income Payment Plan Plus — is the only one that changes what you owe every month, for as long as you stay enrolled and eligible.

Mechanically it is not a discount on the bill. It is a substitute for the bill. You pay a fixed percentage of gross household income, and, in the state’s words, “the balance of your utility bill is subsidized by the state of Ohio.” A January that would have produced a $310 gas bill produces the same PIPP payment as a mild October.

Which companies participate is decided by regulation, not by the company. As of April 6, 2026 the program is administered by the Ohio Department of Job and Family Services rather than the Department of Development — ODJFS states there is “no interruption of service or change in the application process” — but the eligibility rule is unchanged: you need “utility service from an electric or natural gas company regulated by the Public Utility Commission of Ohio.” In Northeast Ohio the regulated natural gas company appears in PUCO’s own filings under the name Enbridge Gas Ohio, the former Dominion Energy Ohio / East Ohio Gas.

Who qualifies: income limit and the regulated-utility requirement

The income test is identical to HEAP’s: at or below 175% of the federal poverty guidelines. For July 2026 through May 2027 the published 12-month figures are $27,930 for one person, $37,870 for two, $47,810 for three, $57,750 for four, $67,690 for five, $77,630 for six and $87,570 for seven. The PIPP table continues to eight members at $97,510.

A caution on larger households: ODJFS’s PIPP page says “For households with more than eight members, add $8,995 for each additional member,” but its own table steps up by $9,940 per member. Those two numbers cannot both be right. If your household is nine or more, do not do the arithmetic yourself — have the provider run the figure.

The second requirement is the one that catches Cleveland renters, and it is the reason this article exists. PIPP is delivered through the regulated-utility rate structure, so it only exists where PUCO has jurisdiction. Ohio Revised Code §4905.02(A)(3) excludes from the definition of “public utility” any “public utility that is owned or operated by any municipal corporation.” Cleveland Public Power is a division of the City of Cleveland — the city describes it as serving roughly 74,000 customers and as the largest municipally owned electric utility in Ohio. It is therefore not PUCO-regulated, and PIPP does not reach it. Neither does the Special Reconnect Order, nor PUCO’s 1/3, 1/6 and 1/9 payment plans.

This matters block by block, not city-wide: within Cleveland’s limits, some addresses are served by CPP and others by The Illuminating Company, which is FirstEnergy and is PUCO-regulated. Neither the city nor PUCO publishes a street-level territory map we could verify, so confirm with your exact address before you assume. Our CPP vs. The Illuminating Company guide covers how to check and how each one opens an account.

How your monthly PIPP payment is calculated

ODJFS gives the formula without hedging: “If your home is heated with gas, you will have a monthly payment of 5% of your gross household income for your natural gas bill, and 5% of your gross household income for your electric bill. If you heat with electric, your monthly payment is 10% of your gross household income… There is a minimum monthly payment of $10.00.”

Either way you land at 10% of gross household income for both utilities combined — the split just changes. Note that it is gross, before taxes, not take-home.

Two practical consequences for a renter shopping for a house in Cleveland. If you are on PIPP, the age of the furnace and the state of the windows stop affecting your bill — the state absorbs that variance. And if you heat with electricity, your entire 10% sits on one account, which means one missed payment puts the whole arrangement at risk instead of half of it.

Arrearage crediting: how on-time payments erase old balances

This is the part of PIPP most people never hear about, and it is worth more than the monthly savings. ODJFS: “Paying on-time and in-full each month reduces your outstanding balance. If you make 24 on-time and in-full payments, your outstanding balance with your utility company will be eliminated.”

Twenty-four is not arbitrary. Ohio’s administrative rules credit a standard PIPP Plus customer one twenty-fourth of accumulated arrearages each time the required payment is made — twenty-four payments, twenty-four twenty-fourths, zero balance. If you walked into this owing $1,900 from a bad winter, two years of on-time PIPP payments retires that debt without a single extra dollar going toward it.

The word doing the work is in full. A partial payment is not a PIPP payment. Paying $80 on a $120 obligation does not buy you a partial credit; it buys you nothing and starts the missed-payment clock.

How to enroll in Cuyahoga County and what to bring

Same three channels as the rest of Ohio’s energy assistance: online with an OHID account through the Energy Assistance Portal, in person or by phone with your local Energy Assistance Provider, or by mail. ODJFS notes a “12-week processing time for both online and mailed applications,” and says elsewhere on the same page that a completed application can take up to 12 weeks to process. Twelve weeks is the only processing time we could find published on the state’s application and HEAP pages, so treat any shorter figure you are quoted as an estimate rather than a commitment. Twelve weeks is close to three months, which is the whole argument for enrolling in September rather than December: a December application is still in the queue while February is being billed.

The state does not currently publish a working page naming the Cuyahoga County provider with its address and phone — the provider detail page returns a 404 and the map is an ArcGIS app. Use the number the state publishes for exactly this: (800) 282-0880, “to be directed to your local provider.”

Bring proof of income for each household member (SSI, SSDI, Social Security, pension, alimony, wages, self-employment, unemployment), copies of your most recent utility bills, a list of all household members with Social Security numbers and birth dates, and proof of U.S. citizenship or legal residency for all members. And note this before you start: the online application “is not currently accessible using… a mobile device such as a smartphone or tablet.” It needs a computer.

One thing you should not have to be told twice: if you are behind and you call your regulated utility, Ohio Administrative Code 4901:1-18-05 requires them to tell you about PIPP Plus and the extended payment plans. If you called and nobody mentioned PIPP, call again.

Staying enrolled: annual reverification and the missed-payment trap

PIPP is not set-and-forget. ODJFS: “Once you are enrolled in the PIPP program, you are required to re-verify your income each year. You must also be caught up on all your PIPP payments by your Anniversary Date, which is the date you signed up for PIPP. Failure to report changes in the household income or household members, missing a payment, or not re-verifying your income can result in being dropped from the program.”

Your Anniversary Date is the date you enrolled, not January 1 and not the start of the heating season. Write it on the lease folder. Two obligations land on it: current payments, and re-verified income.

Reporting changes cuts both ways. A household member moving out lowers household size and can lower your payment. Someone moving in raises both. Reporting late is what gets people removed — not the change itself.

The interaction to plan around: ODJFS lists PIPP enrollment among the factors setting your HEAP amount, and states that “those on both HEAP and PIPP receive a 75% HEAP reduction.” Being on both is allowed and usually correct — but your HEAP credit drops to a quarter. PIPP is still the better instrument because it works twelve times a year.

Winter Crisis is also the safety net for PIPP. Its eligible situations expressly include households that “have PIPP default” or “need to pay first PIPP.” If the thing standing between you and enrollment is the first payment, that is a crisis appointment, not a dead end.

Renters: what to do when the account is in the landlord’s name

PIPP is a payment arrangement attached to a utility account, so it follows the account holder. If the gas or electric is in your landlord’s name, there is no version of PIPP that pays your share — get the account transferred into your name at move-in. Our utility setup guide for Cleveland renters covers the order to do that in, and what utilities are typically included in Cleveland rent covers who normally holds which account here.

Two related rules that are published. For HEAP and crisis benefits, Ohio’s State Plan lets renters whose bill is in the landlord’s name qualify by producing “a lease or written documentation from the landlord verifying that the renter is responsible for the electric bill, and/or the gas bill” — that is a benefit rule, not a PIPP workaround. And for deposits, no PUCO-regulated company may demand more than “one-twelfth of the estimated charge… for the ensuing twelve months, plus thirty per cent of the monthly estimated charge,” with interest of at least 3% a year if they hold it 180 days or longer. That cap is real leverage at move-in — and, again, it does not bind Cleveland Public Power.

Voucher holders should read this alongside the Section 8 utility allowance, since the allowance assumes you hold the account.

Graduate PIPP and what happens when your income changes

If your income rises past 175% FPG, or you choose to leave, Ohio has a landing strip rather than a cliff: graduate PIPP plus, set out in Ohio Administrative Code 4901:1-18-14. Under the rule, each time a graduate PIPP plus customer makes the required payment or more, the company reduces the account arrearage by the difference between the required payment and the current monthly bill plus one-twelfth of accumulated arrearages.

The same rule credits a standard PIPP Plus customer one twenty-fourth of accumulated arrearages per required payment, so the arrearage-credit portion on graduate PIPP plus is twice as large. That is the size of one credit, not a plan length: the rule states no fixed number of payments that zeroes a graduate balance, and it does not define the program in a single sentence. Ask your utility for the written terms of your specific plan rather than planning your exit around a number the rule never gives.

Frequently asked questions

What is the income limit for PIPP Plus in Ohio?
Household income must be at or below 175% of the federal poverty guidelines — the same threshold HEAP uses. For July 2026 through May 2027 that is $27,930 a year for one person, $37,870 for two, $47,810 for three, $57,750 for four, and $87,570 for seven. PIPP Plus adds a second requirement HEAP does not have: your electric or natural gas company must be regulated by the Public Utilities Commission of Ohio.
How much will I pay per month on PIPP Plus?
ODJFS states it directly: if your home is heated with gas, you pay 5% of gross household income toward the gas bill and 5% toward the electric bill. If you heat with electricity, your payment is 10% of gross household income. The state of Ohio subsidizes the balance. There is a minimum monthly payment of $10.00.
Can I be on PIPP Plus and HEAP at the same time?
Yes, but your HEAP credit shrinks. ODJFS lists PIPP enrollment as one of the factors that sets your HEAP benefit amount and states that those on both HEAP and PIPP receive a 75% HEAP reduction. PIPP is usually still the better arrangement because it lowers every month’s bill, but do not budget for a full HEAP credit on top of it.
What happens if I miss a PIPP Plus payment?
You can be dropped. ODJFS warns that failure to report changes in household income or household members, missing a payment, or not re-verifying your income can result in being dropped from the program. You must also be caught up on all PIPP payments by your Anniversary Date, the date you originally signed up. A PIPP default and the first PIPP payment are both listed as qualifying reasons for Winter Crisis assistance.
Does PIPP Plus work with Cleveland Public Power?
No. PIPP Plus requires service from an electric or natural gas company regulated by the Public Utilities Commission of Ohio, and Ohio Revised Code §4905.02(A)(3) excludes a public utility owned or operated by any municipal corporation from that definition. Cleveland Public Power is a division of the City of Cleveland, so it is not PUCO-regulated and PIPP does not apply to a CPP account. Call CPP Customer Care at (216) 664-4600 about their own arrangements.
