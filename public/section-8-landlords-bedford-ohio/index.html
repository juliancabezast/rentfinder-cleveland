Section 8 Landlords in Bedford: Small-Town Paperwork Inside the CMHA System

Bedford is one of 17 Cuyahoga County suburbs where CMHA must collect a Certificate of Occupancy and/or Rental Registration with the Request for Tenancy Approval. The City issues that document, not CMHA — and neighboring Bedford Heights is a separate entry on the same list.

Two Bedfords, two city halls, one CMHA list

Start with what CMHA is not. There is no housing-authority license to apply for — "CMHA does not have a pre-approval process for landlords or units" — and its Administrative Plan agrees: "The PHA does not formally approve an owner to participate in the HCV program."

What CMHA collects on a Bedford address is a document City Hall issues: a Certificate of Occupancy and/or Rental Registration, with the RFTA, for units in 17 named suburbs — Bedford, Bedford Heights, Brook Park, Cleveland Heights, Cuyahoga Heights, East Cleveland, Euclid, Fairview Park, Garfield Heights, Lakewood, Lyndhurst, Maple Heights, Parma, Shaker Heights, South Euclid, University Heights and Warrensville Heights.

Read that list slowly. Bedford and Bedford Heights are two entries because they are two municipalities with two sets of offices, and the owner holding houses across the county's southeast corner is exactly who requests the right paper from the wrong building. Confirm the parcel's municipality first: which Cuyahoga suburbs require what. Cleveland is handled the other way round — since October 1, 2025, CMHA "will verify active City of Cleveland rental registrations through the City's Citizen Access Portal." Cleveland owners get checked; Bedford owners attach.

What we will not print about Bedford's requirements

You can find pages quoting a Bedford fee, renewal cadence and turnaround time. We will not be one of them. CMHA publishes the requirement, not the municipal detail behind it, and no consolidated Greater Cleveland list of who requires what exists to check against. Call the City before you advertise — the one number nobody publishes is how many days Bedford needs, and those days fall outside CMHA's clocks and inside your vacancy. What these documents are: registration and occupancy certificates.

Which clock starts when

CMHA publishes policy targets, not turnaround statistics, and each is written against a triggering event. Inspection, decision and notice come "within 15 days of submission of the Request for Tenancy Approval (RTA)," the clock suspended while the unit is unavailable; the determination follows "within 10 business days of receiving all required information." Neither starts when you apply to the City.

The applicant's clock is tighter. CMHA tells landlords to check the voucher's expiration, "180 days from the issue date" — the RFTA and lease must be in by then — and "[t]he family may not submit, and the PHA will not process, more than one (1) RTA at a time." CMHA accepts no corrections by phone.

The document is asked for twice, and the second time is your first check

Most owners file the registration away as intake paperwork. CMHA names it again when describing what must land before money moves: "you will be paid on the next scheduled payment date after HCVP has received three copies of the signed contracts, the executed lease and occupancy permit, when applicable."

Payment dates are "generally on the 3rd and 16th of each month," and a lease term beginning after the first of the month is "prorated for a partial month." A certificate that is missing, expired or in a name no longer matching the deed costs you at intake and again at the pay run. CMHA verifies ownership through the County Auditor's website, so deed, city document and the required IRS form W-9 should carry one name.

The City's document and CMHA's inspection are two different gates

Whatever Bedford requires before issuing its paper is Bedford's process, and we will not describe what the City has not published. CMHA's is published: the inspection is a separate numbered step in its seven-step path, run by an outside firm against the 13 key housing quality aspects.

The items that fail a unit outright are cheap to fix beforehand. CMHA requires correction within 24 hours of a missing or inoperable smoke or carbon monoxide detector, range burners that do not ignite, no water, no electricity, no heat (October 15 to April 15), sewer back-up, gas leak, and entry doors with broken locks. On a pre-1978 house cited for defective paint where a child under 6 will live, you have 10 days from the initial move-in inspection to produce a passed lead clearance test.

Nobody in Bedford can require you to do this

The U.S. Commission on Civil Rights' Ohio Advisory Committee puts it plainly: "Source-of-income discrimination is legal in most of Ohio," and the federal Fair Housing Act "does not prohibit landlords from refusing to accept tenant applications based on a tenant's lawful income source." Five Cuyahoga County municipalities ban it — Cleveland Heights, University Heights, South Euclid, Warrensville Heights and Linndale — and Bedford is not among them.

So the case has to be that it is worth doing. Verifiable: roughly 15,000 voucher holders countywide, 20,000 households on CMHA's waiting list, and CMHA's testimony that only about 50% of admitted households leased before their voucher expired. CMHA's owner pitch promises "[s]table, guaranteed rent payments deposited directly into your account" — that word is CMHA's, not ours: no unit is approved until it passes, no contract exists until it is executed, and "HCVP does not pay for tenant caused damages."

Frequently asked questions

Does Bedford need a certificate before CMHA will process my RFTA?
CMHA must collect a Certificate of Occupancy and/or Rental Registration with the Request for Tenancy Approval for units in 17 named suburbs, Bedford among them. The City issues it; CMHA does not, and has no landlord application or pre-approval process of its own.
Is Bedford Heights covered by the same requirement?
Bedford Heights appears on CMHA's list as its own entry, separate from Bedford. They are two municipalities, so the document must come from the city where the unit actually sits. Check the parcel's municipality before requesting anything from either city hall.
How much time does the city document add to my lease-up?
CMHA publishes no figure, because that time sits outside its clocks. Its targets start at submission of the RFTA — inspect, decide and notify within 15 days — and at receipt of all required information, for a determination within 10 business days. Anything before that is vacancy.
Does the City's paperwork mean my unit passes CMHA's inspection?
They are separate gates. The inspection is its own step in CMHA's seven-step path, run by an outside firm against the 13 key housing quality aspects. Detectors, working range burners, water, electricity, heat between October 15 and April 15, and secure locks sit on the 24-hour correction list.
Do I have to accept a housing voucher in Bedford?
No. Ohio has no statewide source-of-income protection, and the federal Fair Housing Act does not cover a tenant's lawful income source. Five Cuyahoga County municipalities ban source-of-income discrimination — Cleveland Heights, University Heights, South Euclid, Warrensville Heights and Linndale — and Bedford is not one of them.
