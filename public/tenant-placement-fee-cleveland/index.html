Tenant Placement Fees in Cleveland: What One Month's Rent Buys

A Cleveland manager's published guide puts local tenant placement at 50% to 100% of one month's rent, and the published schedules bear that out — from 50% with a $500 minimum up to a full month with an $800 minimum. One firm adds $250 for a voucher tenant. We charge one month's rent, only after placement.

The number, and where it comes from

A tenant placement fee — leasing fee, lease-up commission, same thing — is the one-time charge for finding a tenant, screening them and getting a lease signed. In Greater Cleveland it is quoted as a share of one month's rent, and the spread is wide.

A Cleveland property manager's published fee guide states that local placement fees "typically range from fifty to one hundred percent of one month's rent," and competitors' pricing pages sit at both ends of that band. Nobody regulates the number — HUD, CMHA and Ohio publish nothing on placement pricing. Everything below is as published July 2026; none of these vendor pages carries an effective date.

What the one-time fee is actually buying

Labor, not advertising. Listing to voucher holders costs nothing: CMHA has partnered with affordablehousing.com and OhioHousingLocator.org to provide free listing options for owners, and asks that ads placed elsewhere say "vouchers are welcome." CMHA does not market units on your behalf. Screening does not move either — "[i]t is the landlord's responsibility to screen prospective HCV tenants just as you would any other tenant." So ask any firm what hours the month's rent represents.

  • Photographing the unit, writing the listing, posting it where voucher holders search
  • Fielding inquiries and filtering them into real showings
  • Running showings, including repeat trips for no-shows
  • Screening the applicant and verifying what CMHA hands over
  • Lease and addenda, signatures, then move-in: keys, deposit, utilities, condition photos

Do voucher placements cost extra?

At one Cleveland firm, in writing, yes: Progressive Urban Property Management publishes a leasing fee of one full month's rent on every managed plan plus $250 for voucher tenants, over a $100–$150 setup charge. It is the only published voucher surcharge we found here — but an unpublished one is still a surcharge, so ask, and get the answer into the agreement. We don't charge one; same month's rent either way. The extra steps behind that surcharge are real, though.

Nobody reimburses you for that work, either. CMHA publishes no signing bonus, damage-mitigation fund or vacancy-loss payment for ordinary voucher landlords. The one advertised cash bonus in the county — "50% of one month's rent" at lease-up — belongs to the Cuyahoga Community Choice Demonstration, and only for its clients in a designated Opportunity Area, checked with its Address Locator Tool.

Submitting an RFTA now requires the owner or designated managing agent to complete CMHA's Virtual Landlord Orientation — once every 24 months, before CMHA can execute a HAP contract on a new tenancy. It runs about 90 minutes, live and virtual. We have already completed it — see what changed on January 1.
  • The RFTA packet. The voucher holder brings it to you. It must be signed by both the family and the owner, though the owner may submit it on the family's behalf. CMHA takes no corrections by phone — deficiencies must be fixed in writing.
  • Local registration. CMHA collects a Certificate of Occupancy and/or Rental Registration with the RFTA in 17 named suburbs, and since October 1, 2025 verifies City of Cleveland registrations through the City's Citizen Access Portal.
  • An inspection before the contract — scheduling, access and any re-inspection.

What the placement fee does not include

A one-time fee is one-time; the recurring money sits elsewhere. Two local firms' guides both land on 8% to 12% of monthly rent, one phrasing it "between eight and twelve percent of collected monthly rent." Published schedules as of July 2026: 10% a month with an $80 minimum for 1–3 units and 8% for 4–9 (Progressive Urban); tiers of 8%, 11% and 12% with $79, $109 and $139 minimums (High Tech); 5% or $129 per unit (B2B); a flat $129 a month (Realty Trust). Ours is 5%, only while the unit is rented — comparison in what Cleveland firms charge to manage.

Renewals are their own line: "one hundred to three hundred dollars per renewal" per that same guide, with published figures of $250 (Evernest), $199 (Realty Trust), $195 (B2B) and $149–$295 by plan (High Tech). Setup and eviction-filing charges live nearby — see the fees under the fee. Our inspection attendance with a written report is $100, or attend yourself and pay nothing.

Is one month's rent worth it? Do the arithmetic

First, discard any Cleveland days-on-market figure you have been quoted, because none is published — RentCafe's index ranks 139 markets and Cleveland is not among them. What exists: nationally, apartments took an average of 46 days to fill in early 2026 at 92.7% occupancy; one Cleveland-area manager advertises a "39-Day Guarantee," a marketing promise rather than a market average; another estimates "two to four weeks annually" of vacancy for most single-family properties.

The cost side you can compute yourself. HUD's FY2026 Fair Market Rent for a two-bedroom in the Cleveland metro is $1,279, so a vacant day is roughly $43 of gross rent never collected ($1,279 ÷ 30 = $42.63) — our arithmetic on a published HUD figure, not an industry statistic. One month's rent is therefore about 30 vacant days: the fee earns its keep if it removes roughly a month of empty time, or a month of your own evenings. Below that line, self-managing the lease-up is a defensible call.

Frequently asked questions

What is a typical tenant placement fee in Cleveland?
A local manager's published guide puts it at fifty to one hundred percent of one month's rent, and the schedules bear that out: Evernest Cleveland lists 50% with a $500 minimum, while Realty Trust Services, B2B and Progressive Urban each list a full month's rent. Figures as published July 2026.
Do I pay the placement fee if the tenant never moves in?
That depends on the firm, and it is the first question to ask. Get the trigger in writing: is the fee earned at lease signing, at move-in, or when the HAP contract is executed? It matters on a voucher deal, because the unit is inspected before that contract exists. Ours is charged only after placement.
Does placing a voucher tenant cost more in Cleveland?
At one firm it does, openly: Progressive Urban publishes an extra $250 for voucher tenants over its one-month leasing fee. The extra work is real — mandatory CMHA orientation since January 1, 2026, an RFTA needing both the family's and the owner's signature, and a Certificate of Occupancy or rental registration in 17 suburbs. We don't surcharge for it.
Is the placement fee on top of the monthly management fee?
Usually yes — they are separate charges. Two Cleveland firms' guides put monthly management at 8% to 12% of rent, and published schedules include 10% a month, tiers of 8/11/12%, 5%, and a flat $129. A 2022 survey of 80 metros found an 8.49% average. We charge 5% monthly while rented, plus one month at placement.
Can I place a voucher tenant myself and skip the fee?
Yes. CMHA states plainly that it "does not have a pre-approval process for landlords or units" and that no application is required, so no fee anywhere buys access. You would advertise, screen the applicant, complete the orientation, submit the RFTA and clear the inspection yourself. A placement fee buys the hours, not permission.
