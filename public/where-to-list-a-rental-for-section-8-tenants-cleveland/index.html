Where to Advertise a Cleveland Rental So Voucher Holders Actually Find It

CMHA points owners to two free listing channels — AffordableHousing.com and OhioHousingLocator.org — and asks that ads placed elsewhere say "vouchers are welcome." CMHA does not market units on an owner's behalf: advertising is step one of its own seven-step path to becoming a voucher landlord, and it stays your job start to finish.

The two channels CMHA names, and both are free

CMHA's answer to "where do I list it" is two destinations, by name: "CMHA has partnered with affordablehousing.com or OhioHousingLocator.org to provide free listing options for owners and landlords." That is the entire official list.

Nothing has to happen first. CMHA "does not have a pre-approval process for landlords or units," and its Administrative Plan agrees: "The PHA does not formally approve an owner to participate in the HCV program." So there is no approved-landlord directory to get into — post the unit the day it is ready.

Advertising is step one of the seven steps CMHA publishes, ahead of Mandatory Landlord Orientation, screening, the RFTA packet, the rent offer, the inspection and the HAP contract. Steps two through seven involve CMHA; step one does not. See CMHA's seven steps.

Why CMHA won't market your unit for you

CMHA draws the boundary in a sentence: if you advertise anywhere else, it asks that the ad say "vouchers are welcome" — and it does not market units on an owner's behalf. Its role is administrative, not promotional. What it runs for owners is back office: a Landlord Portal, a 16-document forms library, an online landlord inquiry form, and a recurring Landlord Working Group on Eventbrite.

There is a budget reality behind that. On August 14, 2025 CMHA asked Greater Cleveland landlords to forgo rent increases over a projected voucher budget shortfall, writing that "to preserve subsidy payment to landlords like you and prevent the termination of Housing Assistance Payment (HAP) contracts... we are implementing various cost-saving measures." It publishes no signing bonus, vacancy payment or damage fund for ordinary voucher landlords either. The marketing spend is yours.

The three words CMHA asks your ad to carry

"Vouchers are welcome" is the phrasing CMHA requests, and it belongs on every channel you use, not only the two free ones — voucher households scan listings for exactly that signal.

The inverse phrasing is the expensive one. Advertising "No Section 8" carries federal Fair Housing Act exposure even where source-of-income discrimination is legal: a 2017 Cuyahoga County audit found housing providers advertising "No section 8" were 26% more likely to deliver unfavorable treatment to Black prospective tenants. That is a race claim, and it does not care that the voucher part was lawful. More: "No Section 8" in your listing.

Geography decides the legal question. As of July 2026, no federal law, no Ohio statute and no Cleveland ordinance makes source of income a protected class in the City of Cleveland, so declining a voucher is lawful there. A few streets away it may not be: PRRAC's March 2026 compilation lists five Cuyahoga County municipalities with source-of-income bans, and Signal Cleveland reports only three — Cleveland Heights, South Euclid and University Heights — specifically include voucher protections, with penalties up to $10,000 plus attorney's fees in Cleveland Heights.

What the rest of the ad should say

Beyond the welcome line, a voucher-ready ad answers what CMHA will ask later, so nobody finds a dealbreaker after the paperwork starts.

  • Bedroom count. If a family takes a unit larger than its voucher size, CMHA applies the payment standard and utility allowance for the smaller of the two — the bigger unit buys no bigger subsidy
  • Rent, plus which utilities the tenant pays. CMHA tests gross rent — rent plus tenant-paid utilities, or the utility allowance — not your contract rent alone
  • An asking rent set against your ZIP code. CMHA sets payment standards per ZIP, not countywide: the FY2026 two-bedroom standard runs from $1,096 in ZIP 44127 to $2,169 in 44113, median $1,525. It caps the subsidy, not your rent
  • Registration status, where your city requires it. CMHA collects a Certificate of Occupancy and/or Rental Registration with the RFTA in 17 named suburbs, and since October 1, 2025 verifies Cleveland registrations through the City's Citizen Access Portal
  • Objective description only. Room count, square footage, heat type, appliances, laundry, parking, distance to a bus line — the housing, never who you imagine living in it

Your ad is reaching people on a 180-day clock

CMHA's Landlord Participation Guide tells owners to check the voucher's expiration date — "180 days from the issue date" — and its unit size, because the RFTA and proposed lease must be submitted by then. Every inquiry arrives with a countdown running.

The pool is deep, the search still hard. CMHA markets "[a] large pool of prospective applicants," serving over 15,000 voucher holders — yet its testimony to the U.S. Commission on Civil Rights reported only about 50% of admitted households found housing before their voucher expired, and Signal Cleveland, measuring a different thing, put it at about 40% within six months. More in how many voucher holders are looking.

Reply speed beats ad copy, for one reason: CMHA policy is that "[t]he family may not submit, and the PHA will not process, more than one (1) RTA at a time." A serious applicant is spending their only live slot on your unit. Meanwhile the vacancy bills you — at HUD's FY2026 two-bedroom Fair Market Rent of $1,279 for the Cleveland metro, about $43 a day ($1,279 ÷ 30).

A third channel, at certain addresses only

The Cuyahoga Community Choice Demonstration, which serves CMHA voucher holders, runs a free listing service through an exclusive unit list plus "Faster processing" of paperwork at the housing authority — paperwork, not inspections. It covers only designated "Opportunity Areas" and publishes no list of qualifying ZIPs — eligibility is checked one address at a time via its Address Locator Tool or (216) 770-4048.

The ad works; the work is still yours

Screening stays with you — "It is the landlord's responsibility to screen prospective HCV tenants just as you would any other tenant" — and the RFTA packet arrives from the household, not the agency. One gate sits between a signed applicant and a paid contract: since January 1, 2026, submitting an RFTA requires the owner or managing agent to complete CMHA's Virtual Landlord Orientation — about 90 minutes, live and virtual, once every 24 months, verified before CMHA can execute a HAP contract on a new tenancy. Existing contracts and payments are unaffected.

Frequently asked questions

Where does CMHA tell landlords to list a vacant unit?
Two free channels: "CMHA has partnered with affordablehousing.com or OhioHousingLocator.org to provide free listing options for owners and landlords." Nothing needs approval first — CMHA "does not have a pre-approval process for landlords or units." If you advertise anywhere else, CMHA asks that the ad state "vouchers are welcome."
Will CMHA advertise my property or send me applicants?
No. CMHA does not market units on an owner's behalf, and advertising is step one of the seven steps it publishes for becoming a voucher landlord. What CMHA provides is administrative: a Landlord Portal, a 16-document forms library, an online inquiry form, and a recurring Landlord Working Group registered through Eventbrite.
Is it legal to write "No Section 8" in a Cleveland listing?
In the City of Cleveland, as of July 2026, yes — no federal law, no Ohio statute and no city ordinance makes source of income a protected class there. The risk is racial: a 2017 Cuyahoga County audit found providers advertising "No section 8" were 26% more likely to treat Black prospective tenants unfavorably.
What asking rent should the ad show?
CMHA publishes payment standards per ZIP rather than countywide — the FY2026 two-bedroom figure ranges from $1,096 in 44127 to $2,169 in 44113. The standard is not a rent ceiling; CMHA calls it "the maximum amount of subsidy." CMHA tests gross rent: rent plus tenant-paid utilities.
