How to Get a Cleveland Unit Ready to Pass a CMHA Inspection the First Time

Start with detectors. Since December 29, 2024 CMHA has required one smoke detector on each level, one in every sleeping area, and one within 21 feet of each bedroom door, all 10-year sealed or hardwired. Then clear CMHA's ten 24-hour emergency items and, on pre-1978 units, any deteriorated paint.

Two attempts, and nothing you can pay to hurry

Passing is step 6 of CMHA's seven published steps to becoming a voucher landlord, and the only one that turns on the condition of the building. CMHA publishes no expedited, fast-track, pre-inspection or self-certification option, so the one lever you control is what the inspector finds.

CMHA's Administrative Plan gives an initial inspection two attempts. A failed unit is rescheduled within 7 to 10 days, and "[w]hen a unit fails an initial inspection the second time, the HCVP applicant/participant is provided with written notice to find another unit." The clocks, in order covers timing; this page is about the unit.

Detectors first — the specification changed on December 29, 2024

Effective December 29, 2024, CMHA requires one smoke detector on each level, one inside each bedroom or sleeping area, one within 21 feet of the door to each bedroom, and one in any living area separated from the bedrooms by a door. Battery-operated detectors must be 10-year sealed and tamper-resistant, or hardwired — a replaceable-battery unit does not meet it.

Carbon monoxide detection has been required since December 27, 2022 wherever there is a gas-fueled appliance, an attached garage or a wood-burning fireplace, and CMHA states that a lack of one "will be considered an emergency fail subject to correction within 24 hours."

The ten items CMHA gives you 24 hours to correct

CMHA publishes a list of violations that must be corrected within 24 hours. Read it as a severity list, not a frequency list — neither CMHA nor HUD publishes deficiency-frequency data for Cuyahoga County, so no honest ranked list of common failures exists. Two Ohio agencies also share the CMHA acronym, so material from the Columbus authority at cmhanet.com does not apply here.

  • A smoke or carbon monoxide detector that is missing or inoperable
  • Kitchen range burners that do not ignite
  • A hazardous gas hook-up for a kitchen range, evidenced by a strong smell of gas
  • No water
  • No electricity
  • No heat, from October 15 to April 15
  • Sewer back-up
  • Gas leak
  • Security issues — unlockable windows within six feet of ground level, and/or unit entry doors with missing or broken locks
  • Anything deemed life-threatening

Room by room, before you call the unit ready

An outside firm does the inspecting, checking each of the 13 key housing quality aspects, and HUD's standard form is HUD-52580 — but 24 CFR 5.707 exempts voucher landlords "from any self-inspection requirements," so there is nothing to file in advance. Pre-1978 paint has its own clock

HUD treats "any deteriorated paint in target housing" as a violation of both HQS and the NSPIRE standards, and 24 CFR Part 35 Subpart M applies where a child under 6 resides or is expected to reside.

If a pre-1978 unit is cited for defective paint and a child under 6 will live there, CMHA gives the owner 10 days from the initial move-in inspection — 30 from an annual — to produce a passed lead clearance test plus proof the repairs were done by a certified lead abatement contractor. Cleveland separately requires owners of pre-1978 rentals to hold a Lead Safe Certification or exemption, valid two years; the lead rules have the rest.

The standard today, and the one arriving February 1, 2027

Voucher units are still inspected under Housing Quality Standards. CMHA's inspections page states the final NSPIRE deadline "has been moved from October 1, 2025 to February 1, 2027," and HUD's September 30, 2025 Federal Register notice extends compliance through January 31, 2027. HUD permits early adoption, so confirm the standard in use at 216.478.9680 before scoping expensive work.

If you are already in the walls, four NSPIRE requirements under 24 CFR 5.703 are worth building to once rather than twice: GFCI protection for any outlet within 6 feet of a water source; a guardrail wherever an elevated walking surface drops off 30 inches or more; permanently mounted light fixtures in the kitchen and each bathroom; and no unvented gas, oil or kerosene space heaters.

If it fails anyway

A life-threatening deficiency must be corrected within 24 hours of notification, any other within 30 calendar days. CMHA reinspects "within five business days of the date the owner notifies the PHA that the required corrections have been made" — that clock starts when you report the work. A second visit is not automatic: 24 CFR 982.405(i) lets a housing authority "accept photographic evidence or other reliable evidence from the owner," so ask.

And do not hand over keys early. CMHA warns "the tenant will be responsible for the full amount of the rent and HCVP will not pay the back amount retroactively." Scoping repairs against the clock covers that work.

Frequently asked questions

How many chances does a unit get at the initial inspection?
Two. CMHA's Administrative Plan says a failed unit is rescheduled for a second inspection within 7 to 10 days, and when a unit fails an initial inspection the second time, the voucher holder is given written notice to find another unit.
What smoke detectors does CMHA require?
Effective December 29, 2024: one on each level, one inside each bedroom or sleeping area, one within 21 feet of the door to each bedroom, and one in any living area separated from the bedrooms by a door. Battery-operated detectors must be 10-year sealed and tamper-resistant, or hardwired.
Do the utilities need to be on for the inspection?
CMHA lists "no water" and "no electricity" among violations that must be corrected within 24 hours, and R.C. 5321.04(A) requires a landlord to supply running water, reasonable hot water and reasonable heat. A unit on shut-off utilities cannot demonstrate its stove, heat or outlets.
Is CMHA inspecting to HQS or NSPIRE right now?
HQS. CMHA's inspections page states the final NSPIRE transition deadline moved from October 1, 2025 to February 1, 2027, and HUD's September 30, 2025 Federal Register notice extends compliance through January 31, 2027. HUD permits early adoption, so confirm with CMHA at 216.478.9680 before scoping major work.
Who schedules the inspection, and who pays for a re-inspection?
Inspections are scheduled at 216.478.9680, with inspection help at 216.271.2116. Under 24 CFR 982.405(h) a housing authority may not charge the owner for the initial inspection, but may set a reasonable fee for a re-inspection where a cited deficiency was not corrected.
