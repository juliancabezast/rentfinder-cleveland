How to Become a Section 8 Landlord in Cleveland: CMHA's Seven Steps, Start to Finish

CMHA publishes seven steps: advertise the unit, complete Mandatory Landlord Orientation, screen the applicant yourself, submit the RFTA packet the tenant hands you, accept the rent offer, pass the inspection, then sign the HAP contract and lease. There is no landlord application — CMHA states it has no pre-approval process for landlords or units.

There is no application to fill out

Becoming a Section 8 landlord in Greater Cleveland is not an approval you obtain — it is a sequence you complete, for one unit and one household. "Please note that CMHA does not have a pre-approval process for landlords or units," its leasing page says; the FAQ adds "No application process is required."

It is optional, too: in the City of Cleveland source of income is not a protected class, so an owner there may lawfully decline a voucher.

Step 1 — Advertise the vacancy

CMHA does not market your unit. It offers two free listing channels — "CMHA has partnered with affordablehousing.com or OhioHousingLocator.org to provide free listing options for owners and landlords" — and asks that ads placed elsewhere say "vouchers are welcome." CMHA told the U.S. Commission on Civil Rights there are roughly 15,000 voucher holders in Cuyahoga County, and that only about 50% of admitted households found housing before the voucher expired.

Step 2 — Complete CMHA's Mandatory Landlord Orientation

Effective January 1, 2026, submitting an RFTA triggers a requirement that the owner or designated managing agent complete CMHA's Virtual Landlord Orientation — once every 24 months, and before CMHA can execute a HAP contract for a new tenancy. It is a live virtual session of about 90 minutes, booked through Eventbrite; CMHA verifies attendance before execution. Existing HAP contracts and current HAP payments are not affected.

Step 3 — Screen the applicant yourself

"It is the landlord's responsibility to screen prospective HCV tenants just as you would any other tenant." The voucher says nothing about rental history. CMHA supplies the family's current address and, if known, current and prior landlord contacts.

Read the voucher itself: CMHA's Landlord Participation Guide tells owners to check the bedroom size it authorizes and the expiration date — described there as 180 days from issue — since the RFTA and proposed lease must be submitted no later than that date.

Step 4 — Submit the RFTA packet

The household brings you the Request for Tenancy Approval — CMHA's step 4 is "Submit Request for Tenancy Approval Packet (RFTA) provided to you by HCV Family." It must be signed by both the family and the owner, and "[t]he owner may submit the RTA on behalf of the family" — we file it, because whoever files it controls whether it is complete. It goes to rfta@cmha.net or the kiosks at 8120 Kinsman Road, and runs to nine documents (what's in it). Four things stall it:

  • Incompleteness. CMHA checks the RFTA against the proposed lease and accepts no corrections by phone — fixes must be in writing
  • A competing filing. A family "may not submit, and the PHA will not process, more than one (1) RTA at a time"
  • Ownership and taxes. CMHA verifies ownership through the County Auditor's website and confirms the unit is not in foreclosure and is current on taxes
  • Local registration. In 17 named suburbs — Bedford, Bedford Heights, Brook Park, Cleveland Heights, Cuyahoga Heights, East Cleveland, Euclid, Fairview Park, Garfield Heights, Lakewood, Lyndhurst, Maple Heights, Parma, Shaker Heights, South Euclid, University Heights and Warrensville Heights — a Certificate of Occupancy and/or Rental Registration must accompany the RFTA. Since October 1, 2025 CMHA also verifies City of Cleveland registrations through the City's Citizen Access Portal

Step 5 — Accept the rent offer

CMHA determines rent after the RFTA is reviewed. Under 24 CFR 982.507(b) the rent must be reasonable against comparable unassisted units — location, quality, size, type, age, amenities, utilities — or the unit is ineligible. The test runs on gross rent, rent plus tenant-paid utilities, not the figure in your ad. On an initial contract the tenant's portion can be no more than 40% of the family's monthly income, or CMHA negotiates the rent down.

The payment standard is not a cap on what you charge — it "is NOT the maximum amount that the landlord can charge; it is the maximum amount of subsidy that the CMHA will pay toward each tenant's rent portion." Standards are per ZIP code, FY2026 figures effective 1/1/2026 for 53 ZIPs.

Step 6 — Pass the inspection

Cleveland voucher units are still inspected to Housing Quality Standards; CMHA's page states HUD moved the final NSPIRE deadline to February 1, 2027. CMHA policy is to inspect, decide and notify owner and family within 15 days of RFTA submission, with the clock suspended while the unit is unavailable. There is no expedited or self-certification option.

You get one retry. A failed unit is rescheduled within 7-10 days, and CMHA reinspects within five business days of the owner's notice. Then: "When a unit fails an initial inspection the second time, the HCVP applicant/participant is provided with written notice to find another unit." Ten listed violations must be corrected within 24 hours.

Step 7 — Sign the HAP contract and lease

Per CMHA's Landlord Participation Guide, the initial lease term must be at least one year, and the HAP contract runs concurrently with it. Then two rules: CMHA "will not execute the HAP contract until the owner has submitted IRS form W-9," and the contract "must be executed no later than 60 calendar days from the beginning of the lease term." Nothing is payable before execution, but a contract signed in time covers up to 60 days of the earlier term. "Any HAP contract executed after the 60-day period is void."

Do not hand over keys early: the tenant then becomes "responsible for the full amount of the rent" and HCVP "will not pay the back amount retroactively." More: What Is a HAP Contract.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need CMHA's approval to become a Section 8 landlord in Cleveland?
No. CMHA states it "does not have a pre-approval process for landlords or units," and its FAQ says "No application process is required." Its Administrative Plan agrees the PHA "does not formally approve an owner" — though it may still deny a specific tenancy.
How long does it take from listing to a signed HAP contract?
CMHA publishes no turnaround statistics, so no honest single figure exists — only targets: the tenancy determination within 10 business days of receiving all required information, the inspection and notice within 15 days of RFTA submission, and a 60-day limit on executing the contract.
Who gives me the RFTA packet?
The tenant. CMHA's step 4 is "Submit Request for Tenancy Approval Packet (RFTA) provided to you by HCV Family." Both the family and the owner sign it, and CMHA policy lets the owner file it — by email to rfta@cmha.net or at the Kinsman Road kiosks.
Is the CMHA landlord orientation mandatory?
For new tenancies since January 1, 2026, yes. Submitting an RFTA triggers it for the owner or designated managing agent: about 90 minutes, live and virtual, required every 24 months, and verified before CMHA executes the HAP contract. Existing contracts and payments are unaffected.
What happens if my unit fails the initial inspection?
You get one retry. The unit is rescheduled within 7-10 days, and CMHA reinspects within five business days of your notice that corrections are done. If it fails a second time, CMHA gives the applicant written notice to find another unit.
