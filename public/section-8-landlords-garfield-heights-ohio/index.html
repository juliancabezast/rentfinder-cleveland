Section 8 Landlords in Garfield Heights: Occupancy Certificates and Turnover Timing

Garfield Heights is one of 17 Cuyahoga County suburbs where CMHA must collect a Certificate of Occupancy and/or Rental Registration with the Request for Tenancy Approval. The document rides in the packet, so every CMHA clock — inspection, tenancy determination, HAP contract — starts only once the city has finished.

The document rides in the packet, not after it

Garfield Heights is on CMHA's list. For units in 17 named suburbs — Bedford, Bedford Heights, Brook Park, Cleveland Heights, Cuyahoga Heights, East Cleveland, Euclid, Fairview Park, Garfield Heights, Lakewood, Lyndhurst, Maple Heights, Parma, Shaker Heights, South Euclid, University Heights and Warrensville Heights — CMHA must collect a Certificate of Occupancy and/or Rental Registration with the Request for Tenancy Approval.

The operative word is *with*. Cleveland is handled the opposite way — "Effective October 1, 2025, CMHA will verify active City of Cleveland rental registrations through the City's Citizen Access Portal" — so Cleveland owners attach nothing. Garfield Heights owners attach, which puts a City Hall document ahead of everything CMHA does. Who requires what: The Cuyahoga County Map.

It then shows up twice. CMHA names an occupancy permit again when describing how a first check moves: you "will be paid on the next scheduled payment date after HCVP has received three copies of the signed contracts, the executed lease and occupancy permit, when applicable." Payment runs are the 3rd and 16th, so a stale certificate costs you at intake and again at the pay window.

What actually starts the clocks, and it isn't the lease

"When can they move in" has no published answer. CMHA publishes policy targets, not turnaround statistics, and every target is measured from a complete packet: the tenancy determination is finished "within 10 business days of receiving all required information" — and all required information includes your Certificate of Occupancy or Rental Registration.

A packet missing it is not a packet on a clock. CMHA will not accept missing information or corrections by phone, so the deficiency gets fixed in writing — days lost while nothing else moves.

One clock is yours to protect: the 15-day initial-inspection window is suspended for any period the unit is not available for inspection. If contractors are still inside when the inspector tries the door, the days stop running. Finish the make-ready first.

Two rules about starting early. They are not the same rule

Owners in a hurry take one of two shortcuts, and CMHA treats them very differently. Handing over keys forfeits the subsidy outright. Starting a lease term early lights a 60-day fuse on contract execution — a fuse that burns while a city office you do not control finishes a document.

When the lease does start mid-month, CMHA prorates rather than skipping: "If a lease term begins after the first of the month, the HAP payment for the first month is prorated for a partial month." The mechanics in full: What Is a HAP Contract.

  • Keys before the approved date. CMHA is flat: "If the owner permits the tenant to move into the unit, the tenant will be responsible for the full amount of the rent and HCVP will not pay the back amount retroactively."
  • Lease term beginning before execution. CMHA may not pay before execution, but executed inside the window it pays for the portion of the term that preceded it, up to 60 days. "Any HAP contract executed after the 60-day period is void."

Your applicant's clock is shorter than yours

CMHA's Landlord Participation Guide tells owners to check the voucher's expiration date — "180 days from the issue date" — and warns the RFTA and proposed lease must be submitted no later than that date. Then the rule that turns your paperwork gap into their emergency: "The family may not submit, and the PHA will not process, more than one (1) RTA at a time." While your incomplete packet sits at CMHA, that household cannot file anywhere else — and CMHA testified to the U.S. Commission on Civil Rights that only about 50% of admitted Cuyahoga County households found housing before their voucher expired.

You need not wait on the tenant to file. The RFTA "must be signed by both the family and the owner," and "[t]he owner may submit the RTA on behalf of the family." Packets go to rfta@cmha.net or the kiosks in CMHA's Main Campus lobby at 8120 Kinsman Road; inspection scheduling is 216.478.9680.

What we will not print about Garfield Heights

No fee, no form number, no processing time, no inspection cadence. We could not source any of it, and no consolidated list of what each Greater Cleveland municipality requires exists. The city's building department is the only authority on the city's own paperwork — call before you advertise, not after a voucher holder hands you a packet.

Be equally skeptical of timelines quoted in landlord forums. Two Ohio agencies use the initials CMHA — the Cuyahoga Metropolitan Housing Authority on Kinsman Road and the Columbus Metropolitan Housing Authority in Columbus. Columbus's incentives and timelines do not apply here.

Nobody here can require you to take a voucher, either. The federal Fair Housing Act "does not prohibit landlords from refusing to accept tenant applications based on a tenant's lawful income source," Ohio's fair housing statute lists no income-source protection, and the five Cuyahoga County municipalities that ban source-of-income discrimination — Cleveland Heights, University Heights, South Euclid, Warrensville Heights and Linndale — do not include Garfield Heights. The case here is worth-it, never obligation.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need the Certificate of Occupancy before I submit the RFTA?
CMHA collects a Certificate of Occupancy and/or Rental Registration with the Request for Tenancy Approval for units in 17 named suburbs, and Garfield Heights is one. Sending it later is not really an option: CMHA will not take corrections by phone, and completes its tenancy determination only after receiving all required information.
Can I let the tenant move in while the city paperwork is pending?
CMHA's position on early keys is explicit: "If the owner permits the tenant to move into the unit, the tenant will be responsible for the full amount of the rent and HCVP will not pay the back amount retroactively." Whether the city permits occupancy is a separate question for its building department.
How long does Garfield Heights take to issue the paperwork?
We will not publish a number, because no source supports one. CMHA publishes targets for its own steps — inspect and notify within 15 days of the RFTA, tenancy determination within 10 business days of receiving all required information — but nothing about a municipality. Timelines circulating online are often the Columbus authority's.
My lease term started and the HAP contract isn't signed. What happens?
CMHA may not pay any Housing Assistance Payment before the contract is executed, but the contract "must be executed no later than 60 calendar days from the beginning of the lease term." Executed inside that window, CMHA pays for the portion of the term that came first, up to 60 days. Executed after it, the contract is void.
