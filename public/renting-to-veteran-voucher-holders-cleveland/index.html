Renting to a Veteran with a Voucher in Cleveland: The Case-Manager Advantage

What is different about renting to a veteran with a HUD-VASH voucher in Cleveland is the assigned VA case manager. HUD-VASH pairs a Housing Choice Voucher with VA case management the veteran must accept, and the VA says that case manager is also a resource for you, the landlord, to help resolve tenancy issues.

The short answer: a voucher that comes with a case manager

HUD-VASH combines HUD's tenant-based Housing Choice Voucher rental assistance for homeless veterans with case management and clinical services provided by the Department of Veterans Affairs at VA medical centers and clinics. The practical difference for you as a Cleveland landlord is that the tenant does not arrive alone: case management is a condition of the voucher, not an optional add-on. A veteran "must agree to participate in case management in order to receive a HUD-VASH voucher," and assistance must be terminated if the family refuses without good cause.

In the City of Cleveland, neither source of income nor veteran status is a protected class, and Ohio has no statewide source-of-income law, so an owner here may lawfully decline a voucher. Nothing below says you have to. It is the case for why a veteran's voucher is often worth saying yes to — built around the one feature a market-rate tenancy lacks: a VA case manager whose job includes keeping the tenancy on track. See our HUD-VASH landlord guide for the details.

What the VA case manager actually does for you

The VA is direct about this. It tells landlords that HUD-VASH and SSVF case managers provide the veteran "education about paying rent in a timely manner, abiding by their lease terms, and maintaining a positive relationship with landlords" — and that they "are also a resource for you, the landlord, to help resolve issues with the tenancy." That is a named professional with a stake in the same outcome you want.

  • A point of contact when something goes wrong. Late rent, a lease-compliance question, or a neighbor complaint can go to the case manager, who already works with the veteran, rather than straight to eviction.
  • Visible in your CMHA tools. CMHA's Landlord Portal lists tenant caseworker information alongside your payment ledger, inspection history, and failed-inspection photos, so you can see who to contact.
  • The subsidy does not end when the veteran stabilizes. A VA determination that case management is no longer needed "is not grounds for termination of HCV assistance." The veteran can graduate from case management and your HAP payments keep coming.
  • Case management travels with the veteran. If it is still required, a HUD-VASH family's moves are limited to jurisdictions where VA case management services are available — the support does not disappear if the tenant relocates.

You still choose your tenant, and you still get paid

A supported tenancy is not a forced one. The VA states plainly that "Screening and approval of Veteran tenants is at your discretion." CMHA's own review of the VA referral is narrow and separate from yours — it may screen only for income eligibility, citizenship, and a lifetime sex-offender registration, and "a PHA may not screen for criminal background other than" that. Your own tenant screening is unchanged.

On rent, the VA tells landlords that with HUD-VASH "a portion of the rent is automatically paid on time by a PHA," and that "Accepting vouchers or subsidies doesn't mean getting less money." CMHA pays its share by mandatory direct deposit — "All owners are required to go on direct deposit to receive HAP payments" — on payment dates "generally on the 3rd and 16th of each month." For how the tenant's share and CMHA's share divide up, see who pays what on a Cleveland voucher. Case management also anchors other supported tenancies in Cleveland; the parallel with Eden supportive housing tenants is close.

How a veteran's voucher differs from a standard one

Most HUD-VASH mechanics match a standard voucher — HUD confirms these vouchers "are renewed based on actual PHA leasing along with all other housing choice vouchers," so the HAP contract works the way you already know. A handful of rules are genuinely different, and each one tends to favor a smoother lease-up.

The rent-ceiling question, handled honestly

HUD's 2024 operating requirements let a housing authority set a separate HUD-VASH exception payment standard of "up to, but no higher than 120 percent" of the area-wide or Small Area Fair Market Rents — above the 90–110% band for regular vouchers — because HUD acknowledges landlords "may be reluctant" to rent to people leaving homelessness. But authority is not adoption: CMHA reproduces that language yet has no policy paragraph enacting a VASH-specific standard, so do not assume a higher ceiling applies in Cuyahoga County. Confirm the number with CMHA before you price the unit; our HUD-VASH exception payment standard guide covers how to check.

The local picture: orientation, inspection, and the VA

Two Cleveland-specific steps apply to a veteran's tenancy just as they do to any voucher. First, effective January 1, 2026, submitting an RFTA triggers a requirement that the owner or managing agent complete CMHA's Virtual Landlord Orientation — about a 90-minute live session, required once every 24 months — before CMHA can execute a HAP contract for a new tenancy. Second, CMHA policy is to complete the initial inspection and notify the owner "within 15 days of submission of the Request for Tenancy Approval."

The VA side of HUD-VASH is delivered through VA medical centers, including the Louis Stokes Cleveland VA Medical Center at 10701 East Boulevard. Veterans themselves enter the program through the VA, not the housing authority — the National Call Center for Homeless Veterans, 877-424-3838, is free, confidential, and open 24 hours a day. Nationally, more than 95,000 formerly homeless veterans were living in homes leased through HUD-VASH as of March 2026, evidence that the model leases up at scale.

A related VA program, Supportive Services for Veteran Families (SSVF), pairs its own housing case management with payments made "directly to the third party to whom rent is owed" and, through its nonprofit grantees, a landlord incentive "equal to up to 2 months' rent" that is "yours to keep." FrontLine Service runs a Cuyahoga County SSVF program; because grantee status is re-competed each year, confirm the current provider first.

Frequently asked questions

Does the VA case manager help me if a problem comes up with the tenant?
Yes. The VA states the case manager is "also a resource for you, the landlord, to help resolve issues with the tenancy," and coaches the veteran on paying rent on time, following the lease, and keeping a positive landlord relationship. CMHA's Landlord Portal also lists tenant caseworker information.
What happens to my rent if the veteran finishes case management?
Your subsidy continues. HUD is explicit that a VA determination that case management is no longer needed "is not grounds for termination of HCV assistance." Case management is required to obtain the voucher, but graduating from it does not end the veteran's rental assistance or your HAP payments.
Can I still screen and pick my own tenant?
Yes. The VA says "Screening and approval of Veteran tenants is at your discretion." CMHA's separate screening of the VA's referral is limited to income eligibility, citizenship, and lifetime sex-offender registration — it may not screen general criminal background. Your own tenant screening process is unchanged by the voucher.
Do I have to accept a HUD-VASH voucher?
No. In the City of Cleveland, neither source of income nor veteran status is a protected class, and Ohio has no statewide source-of-income protection, so an owner here may lawfully decline. Note that a few Cuyahoga suburbs — Cleveland Heights, South Euclid, and University Heights — have enacted source-of-income ordinances that include housing vouchers, so confirm the local rule before you decline a voucher in one of those cities.
Is HUD-VASH rent higher than a standard voucher?
Federal rules allow a housing authority to set a HUD-VASH exception payment standard up to 120% of Fair Market Rent, above the usual 90–110% band. CMHA recites that authority but has not confirmed adopting a VASH-specific standard, so confirm the figure with CMHA before pricing. Our exception payment standard guide covers how to check.
How does a veteran get connected to my unit?
Veterans enter HUD-VASH through the VA, not the housing authority — the National Call Center for Homeless Veterans (877-424-3838) is the free, 24-hour entry point. Once referred and issued a voucher, the veteran searches for housing and applies to you. You screen and approve like any applicant, then submit the RFTA to CMHA.
